\hypertarget{the-model-of-the-two-folders-that-are-to-be-repeated}{%
\section{2. The model of the two folders that are to be
repeated}\label{the-model-of-the-two-folders-that-are-to-be-repeated}}

The two folders study a sixteen-component Dirac field \(\psi\) on two
eight-dimensional spacetimes with the coordinates \(x0,\dots,x7\):
\(x1,x2,x3\) are ordinary space, \(x4\) is the ordinary time, \(x0\) is
a hidden space-like coordinate, and \(x5,x6,x7\) are extra coordinates,
time-like on the \textbf{4+4} spacetime and space-like on the
\textbf{7+1} spacetime. Their hypotheses, in the author's formulas:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Deflation.} The coordinates \(x5,x6,x7\) deflate exponentially:
  the metric is diagonal, and its components along them are
  \(g^{(4+4)}_{tt}=-\mathrm{const}_t^{\,2}\,e^{-2\,\mathrm{rate}_t\,x4}\)
  and
  \(g^{(7+1)}_{tt}=+\mathrm{const}_t^{\,2}\,e^{-2\,\mathrm{rate}_t\,x4}\),
  \(t=5,6,7\), with positive constants and the positive rates
  \(\mathrm{rate}_5=\mathrm{g55}\), \(\mathrm{rate}_6=\mathrm{g66}\),
  \(\mathrm{rate}_7=\mathrm{g77}\).
\item
  \emph{The Lagrangian.} It contains the kinetic term, a mass term with
  the bare Dirac mass \(m_D\), written \(-m_D\,\bar\psi\psi\) with
  \(\bar\psi=\psi^\dagger\beta\), and the self-interaction
  \[\mathcal L_{\rm self}=-\tfrac12\sum_{r=0}^{8}g_r\,I_r,\qquad
  I_r=\sum_{|K|=r}\big(\bar\psi\,\Theta_K\,\psi\big)\big(\bar\psi\,\Theta^K\,\psi\big),\]
  with nine couplings \(g_r\) and the 256 products \(\Theta_K\) of Dirac
  matrices (Sec. 3). On the 4+4 spacetime the self-interaction has two
  forms: \emph{form Q} uses the bilinears \(\bar\psi\Theta_K\psi\) as
  written; \emph{form C} uses the bilinears \(\tilde\psi\Theta_K\psi\)
  of the covariant adjoint \(\tilde\psi=\psi^\dagger C^-\). In the
  folder \texttt{Revision\_Deflation\_CJT\_and\_Kohn-Sham} form Q is the
  definition; in the folder
  \texttt{Revision\_Deflation\_CJT\_and\_Kohn-Sham\_definition\_C} form
  C is the definition and form Q the alternative. On the 7+1 spacetime
  there is only form Q.
\item
  \emph{The Vorton-Scalar.} A complex scalar field \(\Phi_{VS}\) with
  the potential \(\lambda_{VS}(|\Phi_{VS}|^2-v_{VS}^2/2)^2\) and the
  Yukawa coupling
  \(-y_{VS}\,\bar\psi\,(\Phi_{VS}P^R+\Phi_{VS}^{*}P^L)\,\psi\). Its
  vacuum value gives the fermions a mass, and its exchange binds two
  fermions into a pair.
\item
  \emph{Two methods.} The effective potential of Cornwall, Jackiw and
  Tomboulis (CJT) and the Kohn-Sham method.
\end{enumerate}

The central object of both folders is the \textbf{singlet superfluid}:
the condensate of the pair operator \(\psi^TC^-\gamma^{0}\psi\), the
vector pair along the hidden axis \(x0\), of fermions that are massive
and that the Vorton-Scalar binds. On the 4+4 spacetime the folder
\texttt{...\_definition\_C} also divides the field into two
\textbf{halves}, \(\psi=\psi_++\psi_-\) with \(W\psi_\pm=\pm\psi_\pm\),
and most of its results are stated for the two halves.

Each folder computes everything with one Rust program, whose commands
are listed in Sec. 5 with the content of each.

\hypertarget{what-right-handed-means}{%
\section{3. What ``right-handed'' means}\label{what-right-handed-means}}

The chirality matrix of the two folders and of the author's notebooks is
\[\chi=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\cdots\gamma^{7}\ \ (4+4),\qquad\chi=i\,\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\cdots\gamma^{7}\ \ (7+1),\]
and the chiral projectors are
\[P^L=\tfrac12(1-\chi),\qquad P^R=\tfrac12(1+\chi).\] They are the
projectors called P\_L and P\_R in the notebook \texttt{4+4.nb} and PL17
and PR17 in the notebook \texttt{7+1.nb}. A \textbf{right-handed} spinor
is a spinor with \(P^R\psi=\psi\), that is, \(\chi\psi=+\psi\); a
left-handed spinor has \(P^L\psi=\psi\). Because \(\chi\) is Hermitian,
has square one and has the trace zero (check \texttt{44-K1},
\texttt{71-K1}), each projector selects eight of the sixteen components.
A right-handed field is a field all of whose values are right-handed
spinors. The instruction is read in this sense: the field of the model
is replaced by a right-handed field.

The Dirac matrices \(\gamma^A\), \(A=0,\dots,7\), are the real
\(16\times16\) matrices of the project, built from the author's
notebooks, with \(\gamma^A\gamma^B+\gamma^B\gamma^A=2\eta^{AB}\),
\(\eta=\mathrm{diag}(+1,+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1,-1)\) on 4+4 and
\(\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,-1,-1,+1,-1,-1,-1)\) on 7+1. The other
matrices of the model:
\[\beta=-i\gamma^{4},\ \ \alpha^j=-\gamma^{4}\gamma^{j},\ \ C^-=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3},\ \ W=i\,C^-\gamma^{4}\qquad(4+4),\]
\[\beta=\gamma^{4},\ \ \alpha^j=\gamma^{4}\gamma^{j},\ \ C^-=\gamma^{4}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\cdots\gamma^{7}\qquad(7+1),\]
with \(j\) any axis other than \(x4\). For a set
\(K=\{a_1<a_2<\dots<a_r\}\) of \(r\) axes,
\(\Theta_K=\gamma^{a_1}\gamma^{a_2}\cdots\gamma^{a_r}\) (\(\Theta_K=1\)
for the empty set); \(r=|K|\) is the \textbf{rank}, and there are
\(\binom8r\) sets of rank \(r\). \(\Theta^K\) is the same product with
the metric applied to each index; it differs from \(\Theta_K\) by a sign
only.

\section{4. What a right-handed field keeps: the calculation and its

\hypertarget{the-method}{%
\subsection{4.1 The method}\label{the-method}}

\emph{The test of a bilinear.} For a field with \(P\psi=\psi\), where
\(P\) is \(P^R\) (or \(P^L\)), a bilinear \(\psi^\dagger X\psi\) equals
\(\psi^\dagger(PXP)\psi\), because \(P\) is Hermitian. It vanishes for
every such field exactly when \(PXP=0\).

\emph{The test of a pair operator.} For an anticommuting field,
\(\psi^TM\psi=\sum_{ab}\psi_aM_{ab}\psi_b\) keeps only the antisymmetric
part of \(M\), since \(\psi_a\psi_b=-\psi_b\psi_a\). For a field with
\(P\psi=\psi\) it equals \(\psi^T(P^TMP)\psi\), and it vanishes for
every such field exactly when the antisymmetric part of \(P^TMP\) is
zero.

\emph{Why the results are what they are.} \(\chi\) anticommutes with
each \(\gamma^A\). Hence it commutes with every product of an even
number of Dirac matrices and anticommutes with every product of an odd
number. If \(X\) anticommutes with \(\chi\), then \(P^RX=XP^L\) and
\(P^RXP^R=XP^LP^R=0\). If \(X\) commutes with \(\chi\) and is
invertible, as every product of Dirac matrices is, then
\(P^RXP^R=XP^R\neq0\). Every statement of Sec. 4.2 that concerns a
bilinear follows from this, and the program verifies each one with the
explicit matrices.

\emph{The program.} \texttt{code/chirality-rs} (Rust; binary
\texttt{chirality}) builds the matrices of Sec. 3 with the library of
the main project, which constructs them from the author's notebooks, and
performs every test in exact arithmetic over the Gaussian integers. It
tests \((1+s\chi)X(1+s\chi)=4PXP\) with \(s=-1\) for the left-handed and
\(s=+1\) for the right-handed field, so that every entry stays an
integer. It makes 29 checks; all pass
(\texttt{data/chirality\_checks.txt}, printed in full in Sec. 4.4).

\hypertarget{the-results}{%
\subsection{4.2 The results}\label{the-results}}

\textbf{(a) No mass and no coupling to the Vorton-Scalar.} On both
spacetimes \(\beta\) anticommutes with \(\chi\) (it is one Dirac matrix
times a constant). Therefore the mass term
\(\bar\psi\psi=\psi^\dagger\beta\psi\) and the two bilinears
\(\bar\psi P^R\psi\), \(\bar\psi P^L\psi\) of the Yukawa coupling vanish
identically for a right-handed field (checks \texttt{44-M1-r},
\texttt{71-M1-r}). \emph{A right-handed field is massless, and it does
not couple to the Vorton-Scalar:} the scalar can neither give it a mass
nor bind two of its particles.

\textbf{(b) The kinetic term.} The matrices \(\alpha^j\) are products of
two Dirac matrices and commute with \(\chi\). The kinetic term of the
positive Lagrangian,
\(\psi^\dagger(i\partial_4+i\alpha^j\partial_j)\psi\), is therefore kept
(\texttt{44-M2-r}, \texttt{71-M2-r}): a right-handed field propagates,
and the Hamiltonian matrix \(-i\alpha^j\partial_j\) maps right-handed
spinors onto right-handed spinors, so that a field that is right-handed
at one time stays right-handed. (The spin connection of the deflating
spacetimes is a sum of products of two Dirac matrices and commutes with
\(\chi\) for the same reason; this consequence of check \texttt{K1} was
not tested separately.) On the 4+4 spacetime the \emph{covariant}
kinetic term \(\tilde\psi\gamma^A\partial_A\psi\) vanishes identically
for every \(A\), because \(C^-\) is a product of four Dirac matrices, so
that \(C^-\gamma^A\) is a product of an odd number; the covariant
bilinear \(\tilde\psi\psi\) is kept (\texttt{44-M3-r}), but it is not
the mass term of the model, which is written with \(\beta\).

\textbf{(c) The self-interaction.} \(I_r\) is a sum of products of the
bilinears of rank \(r\). If all of them vanish, \(I_r\) vanishes. In
form Q (\(\bar\psi=\psi^\dagger\beta\)) the bilinear
\(\bar\psi\Theta_K\psi\) is kept exactly when \(\beta\Theta_K\) commutes
with \(\chi\), that is, for odd rank. In form C
(\(\tilde\psi=\psi^\dagger C^-\), 4+4) it is kept exactly for even rank.

\textbf{(e) The halves.} On the 4+4 spacetime \(W\) anticommutes with
\(\chi\); it maps every right-handed spinor onto a left-handed one. A
right-handed field therefore lies in neither half \(W=+1\) nor \(W=-1\),
and the division of the field into two halves does not apply to it
(\texttt{44-M4-r}).

\textbf{(f) The left-handed field.} Every test gives the same result for
the left-handed field (checks with the ending \texttt{-l}, and check
\texttt{X1}, which compares all the tables of the two chiralities). The
matrix \(\gamma^{1}\), which represents the reflection \(x1\to-x1\) on
spinors, maps right-handed spinors onto left-handed ones, and
left-handed onto right-handed (\texttt{44-X0}, \texttt{71-X0}). The
reflection leaves the metric of the deflating spacetimes, the kinetic
term and the self-interaction unchanged: the self-interaction is a sum
of squares of bilinears, and a bilinear changes at most its sign.
(Combined with the replacement of \(\Phi_{VS}\) by its complex conjugate
it also leaves the Yukawa coupling unchanged.) It therefore maps the
model of a right-handed field onto the model of a left-handed field, and
every result for the left-handed field is the mirror image of the result
for the right-handed field. This argument was not tested beyond the

\hypertarget{why-the-instruction-cannot-be-followed-as-it-stands-and-the-decision-that-is-the-authors}{%
\section{6. Why the instruction cannot be followed as it stands, and the
decision that is the
author's}\label{why-the-instruction-cannot-be-followed-as-it-stands-and-the-decision-that-is-the-authors}}

The two folders are studies of one physical question: a superfluid of
the hidden-axis pair of massive fermions, bound by the exchange of the
Vorton-Scalar and by the self-interaction, on deflating spacetimes. For
a right-handed field, on both spacetimes and in both forms, the three
ingredients of that question vanish identically: the mass, the coupling
to the Vorton-Scalar, and the hidden-axis pair (Sec. 4.2 a, d). The
left-handed field behaves in the same way (Sec. 4.2 f).

To ``repeat each and every instruction, calculation, analysis, document,
manuscript and provenance, in entirety'' for such a field would mean one
of two things, neither of which is a repetition:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  to write manuscripts with the same sections whose central calculations
  have the result zero or have no object (the gap, the bound pair, the
  superfluid, the thresholds along the clock, the Kohn-Sham mass, the
  two halves); or
\item
  to replace them by calculations that the two folders do not contain:
  pairing in the 28 two-form channels by the kept contact couplings,
  matter of massless chiral fermions, the Fierz algebra of an
  eight-component field.
\end{itemize}

Which of these, or which other model, is wanted changes the hypotheses
of the work. That choice is the author's. The possibilities are:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{(A) The right-handed field as it is.} The model becomes a
  massless eight-component field with the kinetic term and the kept
  invariants of Sec. 4.2 (c). Every calculation of the two folders is
  repeated for it. Where a calculation has no object, the documents say
  so and give the exact reason. The pairing is studied in the 28
  two-form channels.
\item
  \textbf{(B) Chiral couplings of the full field.} The sixteen-component
  field keeps its mass, and the couplings are made chiral, for example a
  Vorton-Scalar or a self-interaction that acts only on the right-handed
  part of the field. (The weak interaction is the known example of a
  coupling that acts on one chirality only, the left-handed one.) The
  author would specify the couplings.
\item
  \textbf{(C) Another model that the author specifies, or no further
  work in this folder.}
\end{itemize}

The work in this folder is paused until the author decides.
