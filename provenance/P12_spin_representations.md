# 1. The two claims, and the verdict in brief

On November 15, 2026, the author asked for the following two claims to be checked,
verified and corrected in a provenance:

> Understand and verify that the 16 by 16 dimensional representation of Spin(4,4) is
> reducible to direct sum of two 8 by 8 dimensional irreducible representations of
> Spin(4,4)\_left and Spin(4,4)\_right.
>
> Understand and verify that the 16 by 16 dimensional representation of Spin(7,1) is
> reducible to direct sum of 8 by 8 dimensional irreducible representations of
> Spin(7,1)\_left and Spin(7,1)\_right.

**Claim 1 (4+4) is correct in substance and needs two corrections of wording.** The real
sixteen-dimensional spinor representation of $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$ is the direct sum of
two *real* eight-dimensional invariant subspaces, the left-handed and the right-handed
spinors. Each is irreducible, over the real and over the complex numbers, and the two are
not equivalent. Corrections: (a) "Spin(4,4)\_left" and "Spin(4,4)\_right" are not two
groups; they are two representations of the *one* group $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$, the
left and right half-spin representations. (b) Neither half-spin representation is
faithful: each sends two elements of the group to the identity, so that the group of
$8\times8$ matrices that each produces is not a copy of $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$ but a
quotient of it by a group of order two, which preserves a symmetric form of signature
(4,4).

**Claim 2 (7+1) is correct only over the complex numbers.** The sixteen-dimensional
representation of $\mathrm{Spin}_0(7,1)$ that the project uses is real, and as a real
representation it is *irreducible*: no real change of basis splits it. Over the complex
numbers it is the direct sum of two eight-dimensional irreducible representations, the
left-handed and the right-handed spinors, which are not equivalent; the right one is
the complex conjugate of the left one, and also its dual. Each is faithful. The same
correction (a) applies: the two are representations of one group. The $8\times8$
matrices of the two parts are necessarily complex.

The rest of this document defines every term, gives the method, the results with the
check that verifies each, the comparison with the four-dimensional case of E. A. Lord,
and the complete output of the program.

# 2. Definitions

*The Dirac matrices.* $\gamma^A$, $A=0,\dots,7$, are the real $16\times16$ matrices of the
project, built by the program from the author's notebooks, with
$$\gamma^A\gamma^B+\gamma^B\gamma^A=2\eta^{AB},$$
$$\eta^{(4+4)}=\mathrm{diag}(+1,+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1,-1),$$
$$\eta^{(7+1)}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,-1,-1,+1,-1,-1,-1).$$
The coordinates are $x0,\dots,x7$: $x1,x2,x3$ ordinary space, $x4$ ordinary time, $x0$
the hidden coordinate, $x5,x6,x7$ the extra coordinates.

*The spin group.* The 28 matrices
$$\Sigma^{AB}=\tfrac14\,[\gamma^A,\gamma^B],\qquad A<B,$$
span a Lie algebra isomorphic to $\mathfrak{so}(4,4)$ or $\mathfrak{so}(7,1)$, the Lie
algebra of the rotations of the spacetime: $[\Sigma^{AB},\gamma^C]=\eta^{BC}\gamma^A-\eta^{AC}\gamma^B$.
The group generated by the exponentials $\exp(\sum_{A<B}w_{AB}\Sigma^{AB})$ with real
numbers $w_{AB}$ is $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$ or $\mathrm{Spin}_0(7,1)$, the identity
component of the spin group; the subscript 0 says that only elements connected to the
identity are meant. Acting on the sixteen components of a spinor, these matrices form the
**spinor representation**, of dimension sixteen.

*Chirality.* $\omega=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\cdots\gamma^{7}$; the chirality matrix is
$\chi=\omega$ on 4+4 and $\chi=i\omega$ on 7+1; the chiral projectors are
$P^L=\tfrac12(1-\chi)$ and $P^R=\tfrac12(1+\chi)$. A spinor with $P^L\psi=\psi$ is
**left-handed**, one with $P^R\psi=\psi$ **right-handed**.

*The charge-conjugation matrix.* $C^-=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}$ on 4+4 and
$C^-=\gamma^{4}\omega$ on 7+1; it defines the bilinear form $\psi^TC^-\psi'$ of two
spinors.

*Terms of representation theory.* A subspace is **invariant** if every matrix of the
representation maps it into itself. A representation is **irreducible** if it has no
invariant subspace other than zero and the whole space, and **reducible** otherwise;
whether it is irreducible can depend on whether real or complex numbers are allowed as
coefficients. Two representations are **equivalent** if an invertible matrix $T$ turns
the matrices of one into those of the other, $T\,\rho_1(g)=\rho_2(g)\,T$ for every group
element $g$. A representation is **faithful** if only the identity element is represented
by the unit matrix. The **commutant** of a representation is the set of matrices that
commute with all its matrices; for the representation of a connected group it is the set
of matrices that commute with all the generators $\Sigma^{AB}$.

# 3. The method

Two theorems reduce the questions to the commutant.

* *Complete reducibility* (Weyl). The Lie algebras $\mathfrak{so}(4,4)$ and
  $\mathfrak{so}(7,1)$ are semisimple. Every finite-dimensional representation of a
  semisimple Lie algebra, and of its connected group, is completely reducible: every
  invariant subspace has an invariant complement. An invariant subspace with an invariant
  complement is the image of a projector (a matrix $E$ with $E^2=E$) in the commutant.
* *Schur's lemma.* Over the complex numbers, a representation that is a direct sum of
  irreducible parts, the $k$-th of which occurs $n_k$ times, has a commutant of dimension
  $\sum_kn_k^2$.

Hence: over the real numbers the representation is irreducible exactly when its real
commutant contains no projector other than 0 and 1. Over the complex numbers, a
commutant of dimension 2, together with two non-zero invariant subspaces, means exactly
two irreducible parts, each occurring once, and therefore not equivalent.

The commutant is the solution space of the homogeneous linear system $XM^{AB}=M^{AB}X$
for the 256 entries of a $16\times16$ matrix $X$, with $M^{AB}=4\Sigma^{AB}=[\gamma^A,\gamma^B]$,
whose entries are integers. The program computes the rank of this system modulo a large
prime. That number is a lower bound for the rank over the rational numbers, so that
$256-\text{rank}$ is an upper bound for the dimension of the commutant; the program then
exhibits that many independent solutions, which proves the dimension. Because the
coefficients are real, the complex commutant has the same dimension as the real one.
Every other check is an identity between integer (or, on 7+1, Gaussian-integer)
matrices, and is exact.

# 4. Results

## 4.1 The 4+4 spacetime

1. The matrices satisfy the Clifford relations with $\eta^{(4+4)}$ (check `44-R0`).
2. $\omega$ is real, $\omega^2=+1$, $\mathrm{Tr}\,\omega=0$; it anticommutes with every
   $\gamma^A$ and therefore commutes with every $\Sigma^{AB}$ (`44-R1`).
3. The commutant of the 28 generators is spanned by $1$ and $\omega$ and has dimension 2
   (`44-R2`; rank 254 of the linear system).
4. $P^L=\tfrac12(1-\omega)$ and $P^R=\tfrac12(1+\omega)$ are **real** projectors of rank 8
   in the commutant: the real representation splits into two real eight-dimensional
   invariant subspaces. Since the commutant is spanned by $P^L$ and $P^R$, each part is
   irreducible over the complex numbers (hence also over the real numbers), and the two
   parts are not equivalent (`44-R3`).
5. In the basis of the matrix files the first eight components are left-handed and the
   last eight right-handed: $\omega=\mathrm{diag}(-1_8,+1_8)$, and every $\Sigma^{AB}$ is
   block-diagonal (`44-R4`). With the blocks $\gamma^A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar\tau^A\\ \tau^A&0\end{pmatrix}$
   of the files, multiplying the block matrices shows that the upper-left (left-handed)
   block of $\Sigma^{AB}$ is $\tfrac14(\bar\tau^A\tau^B-\bar\tau^B\tau^A)$ and the lower-right
   (right-handed) block is $\tfrac14(\tau^A\bar\tau^B-\tau^B\bar\tau^A)$.
6. The 28 generators remain linearly independent on each part: the whole Lie algebra
   acts faithfully on each part (`44-R6`).
7. The form $\psi^TC^-\psi'$ is invariant, $C^-$ is symmetric with $(C^-)^2=1$, and it
   commutes with $\chi$: it pairs each part with itself, and on each part it has the
   eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$ four times each. Each part therefore carries an invariant
   symmetric form of signature (4,4), and the matrices of each part lie in the group
   $SO(4,4)$ of that form (`44-R7`).
8. $\omega=(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1})(\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3})(\gamma^{4}\gamma^{5})(\gamma^{6}\gamma^{7})$,
   and each factor squares to $-1$, so that each factor equals
   $\exp(\tfrac\pi2\gamma^a\gamma^b)=\exp(\pi\Sigma^{ab})$: $\omega$ and $-\omega$ belong to
   $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$. On the left part $\omega=-1$; hence the element $-\omega$ is
   represented on the left part by the unit matrix, and the element $\omega$ on the
   right part (`44-R8`). **Neither part is a faithful representation of the group**; the
   sixteen-dimensional sum is. The group of $8\times8$ matrices produced by the left part
   is $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)/\{1,-\omega\}$, that of the right part
   $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)/\{1,\omega\}$. Each has the dimension 28 of $SO(4,4)$ and lies in
   it (item 7). (That each is all of the identity component of $SO(4,4)$, and that
   $\pm1,\pm\omega$ are all the central elements, are known facts, the "triality" of
   eight-dimensional rotations; they were not computed.)

## 4.2 The 7+1 spacetime

1. The Clifford relations hold with $\eta^{(7+1)}$ (`71-R0`).
2. $\omega$ is real, $\omega^2=-1$, $\mathrm{Tr}\,\omega=0$; it commutes with every
   $\Sigma^{AB}$ (`71-R1`).
3. The commutant is spanned by $1$ and $\omega$, of dimension 2 (`71-R2`).
4. Because $\omega^2=-1$, the real commutant $\{a+b\,\omega\}$ is a copy of the complex
   numbers: $(a+b\omega)^2=a+b\omega$ has only the solutions 0 and 1. It contains no
   projector other than 0 and 1, and **the real sixteen-dimensional representation is
   irreducible over the real numbers** (`71-R3`).
5. Over the complex numbers, $P^L=\tfrac12(1-i\omega)$ and $P^R=\tfrac12(1+i\omega)$ split it
   into two eight-dimensional invariant subspaces, which by item 3 are irreducible and
   not equivalent (`71-R3`). In the basis of the files the generators are not
   block-diagonal (`71-R4`).
6. Because $\omega$ and the generators are real, complex conjugation maps the left part
   onto the right part and commutes with the group: **the right part is the complex
   conjugate of the left part** (`71-R5`).
7. The generators remain independent on each part (`71-R6`).
8. The invariant form $\psi^TC^-\psi'$ pairs the left part with the right part, because
   $C^-$ anticommutes with $\chi$: the right part is also the **dual** of the left part
   (`71-R7`).
9. $\omega$ anticommutes with every $\gamma^A$, so that it represents the inversion of
   all eight axes; that inversion reverses the direction of time, and $\omega$ is
   therefore not in $\mathrm{Spin}_0(7,1)$. The only central elements of
   $\mathrm{Spin}_0(7,1)$ are $+1$ and $-1$, and $-1$ is represented by $-1$ on each part:
   **each part is faithful** (`71-R8`; the statement about the central elements is a
   known fact, not computed).

## 4.3 Comparison with the four-dimensional case (Lord 1971)

E. A. Lord, *General relativity from gauge invariance*, Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 69, 423
(1971), works on the four-dimensional spacetime of signature (1,3). There the Dirac
spinor belongs to the direct sum of two two-dimensional representations of $SL(2,C)$,
the double cover of the Lorentz group: one in which a spinor transforms with a unimodular
$2\times2$ matrix, and one in which it transforms with the inverse of the Hermitian
conjugate of that matrix. In the Weyl representation the $4\times4$ transformation is
block-diagonal with these two blocks; the two representations are not equivalent, and
each is faithful. The 7+1 case is the analogue: two complex parts, conjugate to each
other, both faithful. The 4+4 case differs: the two parts are real and dual to
themselves, and neither is faithful.

# 5. The corrected statements

> **Claim 1, corrected.** The sixteen-dimensional real spinor representation of
> $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)$ is the direct sum of two real eight-dimensional representations
> of the same group, the left-handed ($\chi=-1$) and the right-handed ($\chi=+1$) half-spin
> representations. Each is irreducible over the real and over the complex numbers, the two
> are not equivalent, and each preserves a symmetric bilinear form of signature (4,4).
> Neither is faithful: the left one represents $-\omega$, the right one $\omega$, by the unit
> matrix, so that the matrix groups they produce are $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)/\{1,-\omega\}$ and
> $\mathrm{Spin}_0(4,4)/\{1,\omega\}$. In the basis of the project's matrix files the left
> part is the first eight components and the right part the last eight.
>
> **Claim 2, corrected.** The sixteen-dimensional real spinor representation of
> $\mathrm{Spin}_0(7,1)$ is irreducible over the real numbers. Over the complex numbers it
> is the direct sum of two complex eight-dimensional representations of the same group,
> the left-handed and the right-handed ones, $P^{L,R}=\tfrac12(1\mp i\omega)$. Each is
> irreducible and faithful, they are not equivalent, and each is the complex conjugate and
> the dual of the other.
>
> **For both.** "Spin\_left" and "Spin\_right" name two representations of one group, not
> two groups. A theory in which the left-handed and the right-handed parts transform with
> *independent* group elements, for example a gauge theory with two independent gauge
> fields, uses a larger group, the product of the two matrix groups, of which the spin
> group is the diagonal; that is a choice of the model, not a consequence of the
> decomposition.
