\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

On the $7+1$ spacetime the symmetry of the charge-conjugation matrices forbids a spin-zero pair of a
single Dirac field: the invariant form of the eight-dimensional spinor of Spin(7) is symmetric, and
Fermi statistics then requires a second, flavour index in the antisymmetric combination. The group that
preserves that combination is $SU(2)$; with the fermion number it is $U(2)_{U2}$, and gauging it with the
covariant derivative~\eqref{eq:D}, modelled on the electroweak theory, gives a consistent,
anomaly-free gauge theory of a Dirac doublet. With the quadratic and quartic self-interactions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L} the self-interacting Dirac equation~\eqref{eq:EL} has, in mean field, the explicit
spinor solutions~\eqref{eq:spinors}, and the effective potential~\eqref{eq:Omega} of the superfluid has
a Mexican-hat minimum that breaks $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$: in the vacuum when the pair coupling exceeds
both the critical value and the scalar coupling, at finite density for any attraction, and, if the
scalar channel wins in the vacuum, by a first-order transition driven by density. The breaking
produces a massive neutral vector boson, $m_{BU2}=g'_{U2}f_{U2}$, leaves the $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons massless,
and creates a neutral scalar, $\sigma_{U2}$, of mass exactly $2|\Delta_{U2}|$ in the vacuum, bound below
the continuum when the fermions have a mass; its propagator is the pairing interaction of the broken
phase. The model is an effective theory below its cutoff; the results that do not depend on the
regularization are listed in Sec.~\ref{sec:status}.
