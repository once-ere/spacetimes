## Abstract

On the eight-dimensional Minkowski spacetime of signature $7{+}1$ a single 16-component Dirac field cannot form a spin-zero pair: both charge-conjugation matrices $C^\pm_{1671}$ are symmetric, so $\psi^{1T}_{U2}C^\pm_{1671}\psi^1_{U2}$ vanishes for an anticommuting field $\psi^1_{U2}$. A pair of total spin zero therefore needs a doublet $\Psi_{U2}=(\psi^1_{U2},\psi^2_{U2})$ in the antisymmetric combination, and the symmetry of that combination fixes a new gauge group, $U(2)_{U2}$, with a covariant derivative modelled on that of the electroweak theory. We give the Lagrangian with quadratic and quartic self-interactions, the self-interacting Dirac equations, their mean-field spinor solutions in closed form, and the effective potential of the superfluid. Its minimum breaks $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$: in the vacuum above a critical coupling, at finite density for any attraction. After the breaking the $U(1)_{U2}$ boson acquires the mass $g'_{U2}f_{U2}$, the $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons stay massless, and a neutral scalar, the amplitude mode $\sigma_{U2}$, appears with mass exactly $2\Delta_{U2}$; its exchange carries the attraction in the spin-zero channel. All 55 statements that the results rest on are machine-checked.

## I. Introduction

This paper answers a question posed by the author of PROJECT spacetimes: can a new gauge symmetry act on a quantized 16-component Dirac field on the eight-dimensional Minkowski spacetime of signature $7{+}1$, with a Lagrangian that is quadratic and quartic in the Dirac field, such that the effective potential of the field has a minimum at which (i) a superfluid of bosonic spin-zero pairs, built from the Dirac field alone, forms; (ii) the new gauge group is spontaneously broken; and (iii) a new electrically neutral scalar boson, which exists only after the breaking, carries the attraction between the members of the pairs?

The spacetime is $\mathbb R^8$ with the coordinates $X=\{x0,\dots,x7\}$, of which $x4$ is the time, and the metric $\eta_{1671}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,-1,-1,+1,-1,-1,-1)$. It is contractible, so it carries a spin structure and Dirac spinors exist; they have $2^{8/2}=16$ components. The fields of this paper are quantized: their components are complex Grassmann operators.

The answer, worked out below and checked by machine at every step, is yes, with one qualification. The three requirements are met by the model of Secs. III to VIII. The qualification concerns the word “gauge” in “scalar gauge boson”: a gauge field is a connection, a one-form, and its quanta are vectors. The two new neutral bosons that exist only after the breaking are a massive vector, $B_{U2A}$, the analogue of the Z boson of the electroweak theory, and a scalar, the amplitude mode $\sigma_{U2}$ of the pair condensate, the analogue of the Higgs boson. The scalar is the one that carries the pairing interaction, in the precise sense of Sec. VIII C.

The electroweak theory ([Weinberg 1967](#ref-Weinberg1967)) is the template. There the Higgs doublet $\phi_{\rm SM}$ of hypercharge $Y_{\rm SM}=\tfrac12$ has the covariant derivative, written with one-forms, $$\mathrm D^{\rm SM}\phi_{\rm SM}=\big(\mathrm d+\tfrac i2\,g_{\rm SM}\,\boldsymbol\tau_{\rm SM}\cdot\boldsymbol W_{\rm SM}
+i\,g'_{\rm SM}Y_{\rm SM}B_{\rm SM}\big)\phi_{\rm SM},
\tag{1}$$ with the Pauli matrices $\boldsymbol\tau_{\rm SM}$, the gauge fields $\boldsymbol W_{\rm SM}$, $B_{\rm SM}$ and the couplings $g_{\rm SM}$, $g'_{\rm SM}$; its expectation value breaks $SU(2)\times U(1)$ to the $U(1)$ of electromagnetism, and the bosons W$^\pm$, Z, the photon and the Higgs boson appear, the Z and the Higgs boson electrically neutral, and both defined only after the breaking. In this paper the covariant derivative acts on the fermions themselves, and the field whose expectation value breaks the symmetry is not elementary: it is a pair of fermions, as in the theory of superconductivity ([Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer 1957](#ref-BCS1957); [Yoichiro Nambu 1960](#ref-Nambu1960)) and in the model of Nambu and Jona-Lasinio ([Y. Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961](#ref-NJL1961)).

Three facts specific to the $7{+}1$ spacetime shape the model. First (Sec. III), both charge-conjugation matrices $C^\pm_{1671}$ of this spacetime are symmetric, so a single Dirac field has no Lorentz-scalar pair; a spin-zero pair needs two fields in the antisymmetric combination, and the group that preserves that combination is $SU(2)$. Second (Sec. IV), the left-handed and the right-handed spinors of $7{+}1$ are complex conjugates of each other, so a doublet of one chirality can pair but cannot carry a gauged $U(1)$ without an anomaly; the gauged doublet is a Dirac doublet. Third (Sec. VII), the theory is not renormalizable: the quartic couplings have the dimension mass$^{-6}$ and the gauge couplings mass$^{-2}$, so the momentum cutoff $\Lambda_{U2}$ is part of the model, and we compute with two cutoffs to show which results depend on it.

## II. The 7+1 spacetime and its Dirac matrices

### A. Coordinates, metric, Dirac matrices

The eight coordinates are $X=\{x0,\dots,x7\}$; $x4$ is the time and the other seven are space-like. The indices $A$ and $C$ take the values $0,\dots,7$, the index $j$ (and a second such index $j'$) takes the seven space-like values $0,1,2,3,5,6,7$, and every sum over $j$ in this paper runs over all seven. We write $x^A$ for the coordinate with the index $A$, $\partial_A=\partial/\partial x^A$, $\vec x$ for the seven space-like coordinates as one vector, and sum over an index that occurs twice in a product. The metric is $$\eta_{1671}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,-1,-1,+1,-1,-1,-1),
\tag{2}$$ with the entries in the order $A=0,\dots,7$; its inverse $\eta^{AC}_{1671}$ has the same entries, and it raises indices, for example $\partial^A=\eta^{AC}_{1671}\partial_C$. The letter $i$ is the imaginary unit.

The Dirac matrices $\gamma^A_{1671}$ are eight real $16\times16$ matrices. Each of them is $\pm$ a Kronecker product ($\otimes$) of four of the real $2\times2$ matrices $$\begin{aligned}
\mathsf 1_2&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},&
\mathsf X&=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\\
\mathsf Z&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},&
\mathsf E&=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}$$ namely (check R3-A14 of Appendix B compares all 256 entries of each) $$\begin{aligned}
\gamma^{0}_{1671}&=-\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf E\\
\gamma^{1}_{1671}&=-\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf X\otimes\mathsf E\otimes\mathsf X\\
\gamma^{2}_{1671}&=\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf X\otimes\mathsf E\otimes\mathsf Z\\
\gamma^{3}_{1671}&=-\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf X\otimes\mathsf 1_2\otimes\mathsf E\\
\gamma^{4}_{1671}&=\mathsf X\otimes\mathsf 1_2\otimes\mathsf 1_2\otimes\mathsf 1_2\\
\gamma^{5}_{1671}&=\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf E\otimes\mathsf 1_2\\
\gamma^{6}_{1671}&=\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf X\otimes\mathsf E\\
\gamma^{7}_{1671}&=\mathsf Z\otimes\mathsf E\otimes\mathsf 1_2\otimes\mathsf 1_2
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}$$ The first factor acts on the outermost pair of blocks of eight components, the last on neighbouring components. They satisfy the Clifford relation of the metric (check R3-A1), $$\gamma^A_{1671}\gamma^C_{1671}+\gamma^C_{1671}\gamma^A_{1671}=2\,\eta_{1671}^{AC}\,\mathsf 1_{16},
\tag{5}$$ with $\mathsf 1_{16}$ the unit matrix of the 16 components; $\gamma^4_{1671}$ is symmetric with square $+\mathsf 1_{16}$, and the seven $\gamma^j_{1671}$ are antisymmetric with square $-\mathsf 1_{16}$ (R3-A2). The matrices of the Hamiltonian, $$\beta_{1671}=\gamma^4_{1671},\qquad \alpha^j_{1671}=\gamma^4_{1671}\gamma^j_{1671},
\tag{6}$$ are therefore real and symmetric, with $\alpha^j_{1671}\alpha^{j'}_{1671}+\alpha^{j'}_{1671}\alpha^j_{1671}=2\delta^{jj'}\mathsf 1_{16}$, $\alpha^j_{1671}\beta_{1671}+\beta_{1671}\alpha^j_{1671}=0$ and $\beta_{1671}^2=\mathsf 1_{16}$ (R3-A13; $\delta^{jj'}$ is 1 for $j=j'$ and 0 otherwise).

The product of all eight, $\omega_{1671}=\gamma^0_{1671}\gamma^1_{1671}\cdots\gamma^7_{1671}$, is real, antisymmetric, has the square $-\mathsf 1_{16}$, and anticommutes with every $\gamma^A_{1671}$ (R3-A3). The chirality matrix is $\chi_{1671}=i\,\omega_{1671}$ (Hermitian, of square $+\mathsf 1_{16}$), and the chiral projectors are $P^{L}_{1671}=\tfrac12(\mathsf 1_{16}-\chi_{1671})$ and $P^{R}_{1671}=\tfrac12(\mathsf 1_{16}+\chi_{1671})$. Because $\chi_{1671}$ is imaginary, complex conjugation exchanges the two chiralities: the complex conjugate of a left-handed spinor is right-handed, and vice versa.

### B. Spin group and charge conjugation

The generators of the spin group Spin(7,1) on Dirac spinors are the 28 matrices $\Sigma^{AC}_{1671}=\tfrac14(\gamma^A_{1671}\gamma^C_{1671}-\gamma^C_{1671}\gamma^A_{1671})$, $A<C$. They are real, so a 16-component field and its complex conjugate transform with the same real matrices: in this basis complex conjugation commutes with Lorentz transformations. Let $\psi^1_{U2}$ and $\psi^2_{U2}$ be two 16-component fields that transform alike (they will be the members of the doublet of Sec. III), and let $\mathsf K$ be a real $16\times16$ matrix. A Lorentz transformation multiplies each field by the exponential of a real linear combination of the 28 generators. The bilinear form $\psi^{1T}_{U2}\mathsf K\psi^2_{U2}$ (the superscript ${}^T$ denotes the transpose) is therefore a Lorentz scalar if and only if $$(\Sigma^{AC}_{1671})^T\mathsf K+\mathsf K\,\Sigma^{AC}_{1671}=0\quad\text{for all }A<C,
\tag{7}$$ for then the derivative of the transformed form with respect to every parameter of the transformation vanishes. The two charge-conjugation matrices $$C^+_{1671}=\gamma^4_{1671},\qquad C^-_{1671}=\gamma^4_{1671}\omega_{1671},
\tag{8}$$ satisfy $C^\pm_{1671}\gamma^A_{1671}=\pm(\gamma^A_{1671})^TC^\pm_{1671}$ for every $A$, hence Eq. <a href="#eq:invariance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:invariance">[eq:invariance]</a>: with the upper sign, for instance, $(\gamma^A_{1671}\gamma^C_{1671})^TC^+_{1671}=(\gamma^C_{1671})^T(\gamma^A_{1671})^TC^+_{1671}
=C^+_{1671}\gamma^C_{1671}\gamma^A_{1671}$, and the antisymmetrized combination gives $(\Sigma^{AC}_{1671})^TC^+_{1671}=-C^+_{1671}\Sigma^{AC}_{1671}$. Both matrices are real, *symmetric*, and of square $\mathsf 1_{16}$ (R3-A4, R3-A5). That they are symmetric is the fact on which the next section turns.

## III. Spin-zero pairs

### A. No spin-zero pair of a single field

*The Lorentz-invariant bilinear forms on the 16 spinor components are exactly the real combinations of $C^+_{1671}$ and $C^-_{1671}$* (R3-A6). Equation <a href="#eq:invariance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:invariance">[eq:invariance]</a> is a homogeneous linear system of $28\times256=7168$ equations for the 256 entries of $\mathsf K$. Its rank, computed exactly in integer arithmetic modulo the prime $2^{61}-1$, is 254; a rank modulo a prime is a lower bound of the rank over the rational numbers, so the solutions form a space of dimension at most $256-254=2$, and $C^+_{1671}$ and $C^-_{1671}$ are two independent solutions. The space has therefore exactly the dimension 2.

The components of a quantized Dirac field anticommute. For a single field, say $\psi^1_{U2}$, with components $\psi^1_{U2\,l}$ ($l=1,\dots,16$), $$\begin{aligned}
\psi^{1T}_{U2}\mathsf K\psi^1_{U2}&=\sum_{l,l'}\psi^1_{U2\,l}\,\mathsf K_{ll'}\,\psi^1_{U2\,l'}\\
&=\tfrac12\sum_{l,l'}\psi^1_{U2\,l}\big(\mathsf K_{ll'}-\mathsf K_{l'l}\big)\psi^1_{U2\,l'},
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}$$ because $\psi^1_{U2\,l}\psi^1_{U2\,l'}=-\psi^1_{U2\,l'}\psi^1_{U2\,l}$: only the antisymmetric part of $\mathsf K$ survives. Both $C^\pm_{1671}$ are symmetric. Therefore:

*Theorem 1. On the $7{+}1$ spacetime a single Dirac field has no Lorentz-scalar pair:* $\psi^{1T}_{U2}C^\pm_{1671}\psi^1_{U2}=0$ identically.

The same holds for the two other Lorentz scalars of the Clifford algebra, $C^\pm_{1671}\omega_{1671}$, which are $\pm C^\mp_{1671}$. Table I lists, for every index set $K=(A_1<\dots<A_r)$ of rank $r$ and the ordered product $\mathsf G_K=\gamma^{A_1}_{1671}\cdots\gamma^{A_r}_{1671}$ ($\mathsf G_K=\mathsf 1_{16}$ for the empty set), how many of the matrices $C^\pm_{1671}\mathsf G_K$ are antisymmetric, i.e. give a nonvanishing pair of one field (R3-A7; each of the 512 matrices is either symmetric or antisymmetric). Pairs of one field exist only with a vector index ($r=1$, for $C^-_{1671}$), with tensor indices, or with their duals; never as Lorentz scalars ($r=0$ or 8). This is unlike the $3+1$ spacetime of ordinary physics, whose charge-conjugation matrix is antisymmetric, so that a single field there has a spin-zero pair.

### B. The doublet and its two scalar pairs

Two fields $\psi^1_{U2}$, $\psi^2_{U2}$ do have Lorentz-scalar pairs, $$\Phi^\pm_{U2}=\psi^{1T}_{U2}C^\pm_{1671}\psi^2_{U2}
=\tfrac12\,\Psi^T_{U2}\big(\mathsf E\otimes C^\pm_{1671}\big)\Psi_{U2},
\tag{10}$$ where $\Psi_{U2}=(\psi^1_{U2},\psi^2_{U2})$ is the doublet (32 components, the doublet index outside) and $\mathsf E$ of Eq. <a href="#eq:twobytwo" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:twobytwo">[eq:twobytwo]</a> acts on the doublet index. The second form follows from $\psi^{2T}_{U2}C^\pm_{1671}\psi^1_{U2}=-\psi^{1T}_{U2}(C^\pm_{1671})^T\psi^2_{U2}
=-\Phi^\pm_{U2}$. The $32\times32$ matrices $\mathsf E\otimes C^\pm_{1671}$ are antisymmetric, while $\mathsf 1_2\otimes C^\pm_{1671}$, $\mathsf X\otimes C^\pm_{1671}$ and $\mathsf Z\otimes C^\pm_{1671}$, built with the three symmetric $2\times2$ matrices, are symmetric (R3-A8): by Eq. <a href="#eq:antisym" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:antisym">[eq:antisym]</a>, applied to the 32 components of $\Psi_{U2}$, the scalar pairs of the doublet are antisymmetric in the doublet index, and there are exactly the two of Eq. <a href="#eq:pairs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pairs">[eq:pairs]</a>.

### C. Which pair has spin zero

In the rest frame of a fermion, its positive-energy components are the eigenvectors of $\beta_{1671}$ with the eigenvalue $+1$, an eight-dimensional space. The 21 rotation generators $\gamma^j_{1671}\gamma^{j'}_{1671}$ ($j<j'$) are real, antisymmetric, and commute with $\beta_{1671}$. The real matrices that commute with all of them form a space of dimension 4, spanned by $\mathsf 1_{16}$, $\beta_{1671}$, $\omega_{1671}$, $\beta_{1671}\omega_{1671}$; those that also commute with $\beta_{1671}$, a space of dimension 2 (R3-A12, with exact ranks as in R3-A6). Hence each eigenspace of $\beta_{1671}$ is an absolutely irreducible real representation of the rotation group Spin(7), the eight-dimensional spinor, and *a rotation-invariant bilinear form on it is unique up to a factor*: two fermions at rest of different flavour form exactly one state of total spin zero. The two pairs differ in how they reach it (R3-A11): $$\begin{aligned}
(\mathsf 1_{16}+\beta_{1671})C^+_{1671}(\mathsf 1_{16}+\beta_{1671})&=2(\mathsf 1_{16}+\beta_{1671}),\\
(\mathsf 1_{16}\pm\beta_{1671})C^-_{1671}(\mathsf 1_{16}\pm\beta_{1671})&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}$$ The first line follows from $C^+_{1671}=\beta_{1671}$ and $\beta_{1671}(\mathsf 1_{16}+\beta_{1671})=\mathsf 1_{16}+\beta_{1671}$; the second from $C^-_{1671}\beta_{1671}=-\beta_{1671}C^-_{1671}$, which moves the projector $\tfrac12(\mathsf 1_{16}\pm\beta_{1671})$ through $C^-_{1671}$ into $\tfrac12(\mathsf 1_{16}\mp\beta_{1671})$. Thus $\Phi^+_{U2}$ joins two positive-energy components at rest: in the non-relativistic limit it is the pair of spin zero and orbital angular momentum zero, the singlet of total spin zero that the task asks for. $\Phi^-_{U2}$ joins an upper with a lower component and vanishes in that limit: it is the pseudoscalar partner (in the non-relativistic language, spin one and orbital angular momentum one, coupled to zero).

The first line of Eq. <a href="#eq:restframe" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:restframe">[eq:restframe]</a> also shows *why* a single field cannot pair. On the positive-energy components at rest the form $C^+_{1671}=\beta_{1671}$ is the unit matrix: the invariant form of the eight-dimensional spinor of Spin(7) is *symmetric*, so the state of total spin zero of two fermions at rest is symmetric in their spinor indices (in three space dimensions the spin singlet is antisymmetric). With a symmetric orbital part (orbital angular momentum zero), Fermi statistics demands antisymmetry in some other index, and the only one available is a flavour index: the pair needs two fields, in the antisymmetric combination $\mathsf E$ of Eq. <a href="#eq:pairs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pairs">[eq:pairs]</a>. The superfluid of this paper is the condensate of $\Phi^+_{U2}$; Sec. VI C shows the consequence of this choice at a Fermi surface.

### D. Chirality

The matrices $(P^L_{1671})^TC^\pm_{1671}P^R_{1671}$ and $(P^R_{1671})^TC^\pm_{1671}P^L_{1671}$ vanish, while $(P^L_{1671})^TC^\pm_{1671}P^L_{1671}$ and $(P^R_{1671})^TC^\pm_{1671}P^R_{1671}$ do not; the mass term $\bar\psi^1_{U2}\psi^1_{U2}=\psi^{1\dagger}_{U2}\beta_{1671}\psi^1_{U2}$ joins a left-handed with a right-handed component (R3-A10): the scalar pairs join two left-handed or two right-handed components. A doublet of one chirality therefore has the pair $\Phi^+_{U2}$ too; a single chiral field has none (its matrix $(P^L_{1671})^TC^+_{1671}P^L_{1671}$ is symmetric).

## IV. The new gauge group and its covariant derivative

### A. The symmetry that the pair selects

Let a $2\times2$ complex matrix $\Upsilon_{U2}$ act on the doublet index, $\Psi_{U2}\to(\Upsilon_{U2}\otimes\mathsf 1_{16})\Psi_{U2}$; such a transformation commutes with Spin(7,1), because it does not touch the spinor index. (Below, a matrix that acts on one of the two indices only is written without the unit matrix of the other: $\gamma^A_{1671}$ acting on $\Psi_{U2}$ means $\mathsf 1_2\otimes\gamma^A_{1671}$, and $\Upsilon_{U2}$ acting on $\Psi_{U2}$ means $\Upsilon_{U2}\otimes\mathsf 1_{16}$.) The kinetic and mass terms of the doublet, $\bar\Psi_{U2}(i\gamma^A_{1671}\partial_A-m_{U2})\Psi_{U2}$, are the sum of the same terms for $\psi^1_{U2}$ and $\psi^2_{U2}$ with the common mass $m_{U2}$, and $\bar\Psi_{U2}=
\Psi^\dagger_{U2}\beta_{1671}$. Under a constant $\Upsilon_{U2}$ they become $\Psi^\dagger_{U2}\Upsilon^\dagger_{U2}\beta_{1671}(i\gamma^A_{1671}\partial_A-m_{U2})\Upsilon_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$, which equals the original for all fields if and only if $\Upsilon^\dagger_{U2}\Upsilon_{U2}=\mathsf 1_2$: the transformation must be unitary. For every $2\times2$ matrix the entries of $\Upsilon^T_{U2}\mathsf E\,\Upsilon_{U2}$ are its $2\times2$ minors, so that $$\Upsilon^T_{U2}\,\mathsf E\,\Upsilon_{U2}=\det(\Upsilon_{U2})\,\mathsf E,
\qquad
\Phi^\pm_{U2}\to\det(\Upsilon_{U2})\,\Phi^\pm_{U2},
\tag{12}$$ by Eq. <a href="#eq:pairs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pairs">[eq:pairs]</a> (R3-A9). A unitary transformation leaves the spin-zero pair invariant if and only if its determinant is 1: the pair selects $SU(2)$. Together with the overall phase, which counts the fermions, the unitary transformations form $$\begin{aligned}
U(2)_{U2}&=\big(SU(2)_{U2}\times U(1)_{U2}\big)/\mathbb Z_2,\\
\Upsilon_{U2}&=\exp\!\big(\tfrac i2\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}\big)\exp\!\big(iy_{U2}\Theta^0_{U2}\big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}$$ with the Pauli matrices $\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}=(\tau^1_{U2},\tau^2_{U2},\tau^3_{U2})$ acting on the doublet index, three real parameters $\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}$, one real parameter $\Theta^0_{U2}$, and the $U(1)_{U2}$ charge $y_{U2}=\tfrac12$ of the doublet. The quotient by $\mathbb Z_2$ removes the double counting of the element $-\mathsf 1_2$, which belongs to $SU(2)_{U2}$ and equals $\exp(iy_{U2}\Theta^0_{U2})$ at $\Theta^0_{U2}=2\pi$. The determinant is $\det\Upsilon_{U2}=\exp(2iy_{U2}\Theta^0_{U2})=\exp(i\Theta^0_{U2})$. The value $y_{U2}=\tfrac12$ copies the hypercharge of the Higgs doublet, Eq. <a href="#eq:sm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:sm">[eq:sm]</a>; with it the pair has charge 1. We make this symmetry local, $\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}(X)$ and $\Theta^0_{U2}(X)$, and call it the new gauge group. The group is the group of the electroweak theory; what is new is where it acts (on an internal index of 16-component fermions of the $7{+}1$ spacetime), why it is there (it is the symmetry of the only spin-zero pair, Theorem 1), and which field breaks it (a pair of fermions, not an elementary scalar).

### B. The covariant derivative

Modelled on Eq. <a href="#eq:sm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:sm">[eq:sm]</a>, the new covariant derivative is $$\begin{aligned}
D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}&=\Big(\partial_A+\tfrac i2\,g_{U2}\,\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol W_{U2A}\\
&\qquad+i\,g'_{U2}\,y_{U2}\,B_{U2A}\Big)\Psi_{U2}\\
&\equiv(\partial_A+i\mathcal W_{U2A})\Psi_{U2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}$$ with a triple of real vector fields $\boldsymbol W_{U2A}=(W^1_{U2A},W^2_{U2A},W^3_{U2A})$ for $SU(2)_{U2}$, a real vector field $B_{U2A}$ for $U(1)_{U2}$, the couplings $g_{U2}$ and $g'_{U2}$, and the Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix $\mathcal W_{U2A}=\tfrac12g_{U2}\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol
W_{U2A}+g'_{U2}y_{U2}B_{U2A}\mathsf 1_2$. It acts on the doublet index, on each of the 16 spinor components alike. Under a local transformation $\Psi'_{U2}=\Upsilon_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$ the connection transforms as $$\mathcal W'_{U2A}=\Upsilon_{U2}\mathcal W_{U2A}\Upsilon^{-1}_{U2}+i(\partial_A\Upsilon_{U2})\Upsilon^{-1}_{U2},
\tag{15}$$ and then $D'_{U2A}\Psi'_{U2}=\Upsilon_{U2}D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}$: indeed $(\partial_A+i\mathcal W'_{U2A})\Upsilon_{U2}\Psi_{U2}=(\partial_A\Upsilon_{U2})\Psi_{U2}+\Upsilon_{U2}\partial_A\Psi_{U2}
+i\Upsilon_{U2}\mathcal W_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}-(\partial_A\Upsilon_{U2})\Psi_{U2}$ (R3-G1). The new connection $\mathcal W'_{U2A}$ is again Hermitian, because $\Upsilon_{U2}$ is unitary, and its $U(1)_{U2}$ part is $B'_{U2A}=B_{U2A}-\partial_A\Theta^0_{U2}/g'_{U2}$ (R3-G2). To first order in $\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}$ the $SU(2)_{U2}$ part reads $\boldsymbol W'_{U2A}=\boldsymbol W_{U2A}-\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}\times\boldsymbol W_{U2A}
-\partial_A\boldsymbol\Theta_{U2}/g_{U2}$ ($\times$ is the cross product of triples); it follows from $\tau^1_{U2}\tau^2_{U2}-\tau^2_{U2}\tau^1_{U2}=2i\tau^3_{U2}$ and the two cyclic permutations (R3-A9), which turn the commutator of two combinations of Pauli matrices into the combination with the cross product of the coefficients.

The commutator of two covariant derivatives defines the field strength, $$\begin{aligned}
[D_{U2A},D_{U2C}]\Psi_{U2}&=i\mathcal F_{U2AC}\Psi_{U2},\\
\mathcal F_{U2AC}&=\partial_A\mathcal W_{U2C}-\partial_C\mathcal W_{U2A}\\
&\quad+i[\mathcal W_{U2A},\mathcal W_{U2C}]\\
&=\tfrac12g_{U2}\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}\\
&\quad+g'_{U2}y_{U2}B_{U2AC}\mathsf 1_2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}$$ with $\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}=\partial_A\boldsymbol W_{U2C}-\partial_C\boldsymbol W_{U2A}-g_{U2}\boldsymbol
W_{U2A}\times\boldsymbol W_{U2C}$ and $B_{U2AC}=\partial_AB_{U2C}-\partial_CB_{U2A}$ (R3-G3, R3-G4). It transforms by conjugation, $\mathcal F'_{U2AC}=\Upsilon_{U2}\mathcal F_{U2AC}\Upsilon^{-1}_{U2}$ (R3-G5), so the traces $\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal F_{U2AC}\mathcal F^{AC}_{U2})=\tfrac12g_{U2}^2
\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}\cdot\boldsymbol W^{AC}_{U2}+2g'^2_{U2}y_{U2}^2B_{U2AC}B^{AC}_{U2}$ and $\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathcal F_{U2AC}=2g'_{U2}y_{U2}B_{U2AC}$ are invariant, and so is the Lagrangian of the gauge fields, $-\tfrac14\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}\cdot\boldsymbol W^{AC}_{U2}-\tfrac14B_{U2AC}B^{AC}_{U2}$.

On the eight-dimensional spacetime the action is dimensionless when the Dirac field has the dimension mass$^{7/2}$ and the gauge fields mass$^{3}$; the couplings $g_{U2}$ and $g'_{U2}$ then have the dimension mass$^{-2}$.

### C. The charges of the pairs

By Eq. <a href="#eq:detE" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:detE">[eq:detE]</a> the pairs are $SU(2)_{U2}$ singlets and carry the $U(1)_{U2}$ charge $2y_{U2}=1$: $\Phi^\pm_{U2}\to\exp(i\Theta^0_{U2})\Phi^\pm_{U2}$ (R3-G6). Their covariant derivative contains only $B_{U2A}$. Since $(\tau^1_{U2})^T\mathsf E+\mathsf E\tau^1_{U2}=0$, and the same for $\tau^2_{U2}$ and $\tau^3_{U2}$ (R3-A9), $\mathcal W^T_{U2A}\mathsf E+\mathsf E\mathcal W_{U2A}
=2g'_{U2}y_{U2}B_{U2A}\mathsf E$, and the product rule gives $$\begin{aligned}
(\partial_A+ig'_{U2}B_{U2A})\Phi^\pm_{U2}={}&\tfrac12(D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2})^T(\mathsf E\otimes C^\pm_{1671})\Psi_{U2}\\
&+\tfrac12\Psi^T_{U2}(\mathsf E\otimes C^\pm_{1671})D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}$$ A condensate of $\Phi^+_{U2}$ is therefore invariant under $SU(2)_{U2}$ and changes its phase under $U(1)_{U2}$: if it forms, it breaks $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$, and only $B_{U2A}$ can acquire a mass (Sec. VIII). In the electroweak theory the Higgs doublet carries both kinds of charge, and the boson that becomes massive is a mixture of the third component of $\boldsymbol W_{\rm SM}$ and of $B_{\rm SM}$; here the order parameter is an $SU(2)_{U2}$ singlet, and there is no mixing.

### D. Chirality and anomalies

In eight dimensions the gauge anomaly of Weyl fermions comes from one-loop diagrams with five external gauge bosons; it is governed by the fifth power of the field strength, and the mixed gauge-gravitational anomalies by its third and first powers multiplied by curvature terms ([Alvarez-Gaumé and Witten 1984](#ref-AlvarezGaumeWitten1984)). For a $U(1)$ the three coefficients are the sums of the fifth, of the third and of the first powers of the charges of the left-handed Weyl components. For $SU(2)_{U2}$ alone the symmetrized traces of three and of five Pauli matrices vanish (R3-B2): the group has no perturbative anomaly of its own.

A Dirac doublet consists of a left-handed and a right-handed doublet. Because complex conjugation exchanges the chiralities on the $7{+}1$ spacetime (Sec. II), the right-handed doublet of charge $y_{U2}$ is equivalent to a left-handed doublet in the conjugate representation, of charge $-y_{U2}$, and the three coefficients vanish (R3-B2). More strongly, the Dirac doublet admits the gauge-invariant mass term $m_{U2}\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$; a regulator field of large mass of the same kind (Pauli and Villars) then respects $U(2)_{U2}$, so every gauge anomaly of the Dirac doublet cancels. A doublet of one chirality, by contrast, has the coefficients $(\tfrac1{16},\tfrac14,1)$ (two components of charge $\tfrac12$; R3-B2): its $U(1)_{U2}$ cannot be gauged. Since the spin-zero pair joins equal chiralities (Sec. III), a chiral doublet could still pair, but it could neither carry the gauged $U(1)_{U2}$ nor have a mass. The gauged doublet of this paper is the Dirac doublet; the chiral doublet is followed in Sec. VII F only to compare its potential.

## V. The Lagrangian and the self-interacting Dirac equations

### A. The Lagrangian

The Lagrangian density of the model is $$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{U2}={}&\bar\Psi_{U2}\big(i\gamma^A_{1671}D_{U2A}-m_{U2}\big)\Psi_{U2}\\
&+G^S_{U2}\big(\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\big)^2+G^P_{U2}\,\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}\\
&-\tfrac14\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}\cdot\boldsymbol W^{AC}_{U2}-\tfrac14B_{U2AC}B^{AC}_{U2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}$$ The self-interaction of the Dirac field is quadratic (the mass term) and quartic: the scalar channel $(\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2})^2$ of Nambu and Jona-Lasinio ([Y. Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961](#ref-NJL1961)), and the pair channel $\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}$ of the theory of superconductivity ([Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer 1957](#ref-BCS1957)). Both terms are Lorentz scalars and invariant under $U(2)_{U2}$: $\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$ is invariant under every unitary $\Upsilon_{U2}$, and $\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}$ is multiplied by $|\det\Upsilon_{U2}|^2=1$. Both are Hermitian. Positive couplings attract (Sec. VII); on the eight-dimensional spacetime $G^S_{U2}$ and $G^P_{U2}$ have the dimension mass$^{-6}$.

These two couplings are not the most general quartic self-interaction allowed by the symmetries; there are further channels (vector, tensor, the pseudoscalar pair $\Phi^-_{U2}$). They are the two channels of total spin zero that compete for the vacuum: the scalar channel favours a mass and leaves $U(2)_{U2}$ intact, the pair channel favours the superfluid and breaks it. A Lagrangian is “interesting” in the sense of the task if its effective potential has a minimum with $\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\neq0$; Sec. VII maps the couplings for which this happens.

A useful identity rewrites the conjugate pair with the conjugate doublet $\tilde\Psi_{U2}=\mathsf E\,\Psi^*_{U2}=(\psi^{2*}_{U2},-\psi^{1*}_{U2})$, where the asterisk on a column of operators denotes the column of their Hermitian conjugates. From $\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}=\psi^{2\dagger}_{U2}C^+_{1671}\psi^{1*}_{U2}$, from $C^+_{1671}=\beta_{1671}$ and from the anticommutation of the components, $$\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}=\bar\psi^2_{U2}\psi^{1*}_{U2}=-\bar\psi^1_{U2}\psi^{2*}_{U2}
=-\tfrac12\,\bar\Psi_{U2}\tilde\Psi_{U2}.
\tag{19}$$ $\tilde\Psi_{U2}$ transforms under $SU(2)_{U2}$ like $\Psi_{U2}$ (because $\mathsf E\,\Upsilon^*_{U2}\mathsf E^{-1}=\Upsilon_{U2}$ for $\det\Upsilon_{U2}=1$, by Eq. <a href="#eq:detE" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:detE">[eq:detE]</a>), under $U(1)_{U2}$ with the charge $-y_{U2}$, and under Spin(7,1) like $\Psi_{U2}$, since the $\gamma^A_{1671}$ are real.

### B. The Euler–Lagrange equations

For anticommuting fields $\Psi_{U2}$ and $\bar\Psi_{U2}$ are varied independently, with the derivative taken from the left. $\bar\Psi_{U2}$ occurs without derivatives; by Eq. <a href="#eq:phidag" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:phidag">[eq:phidag]</a>, $\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}=-\tfrac12\bar\Psi_{U2}(\mathsf E\otimes\beta_{1671})
\bar\Psi^T_{U2}$, whose derivative with respect to $\bar\Psi_{U2}$ is $-\tilde\Psi_{U2}$ (the matrix $\mathsf E\otimes\beta_{1671}$ is antisymmetric, and the two terms of the product rule are equal). The Euler–Lagrange equation $\partial\mathcal L_{U2}/\partial\bar\Psi_{U2}=0$ is the self-interacting Dirac equation $$\begin{aligned}
i\gamma^A_{1671}D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}={}&\big(m_{U2}-2G^S_{U2}\,\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\big)\Psi_{U2}\\
&+G^P_{U2}\,\Phi^+_{U2}\,\tilde\Psi_{U2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}$$ or, member by member, $$\begin{aligned}
\big(i\gamma^A_{1671}D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}\big)^1={}&\big(m_{U2}-2G^S_{U2}\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\big)\psi^1_{U2}\\
&+G^P_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}\,\psi^{2*}_{U2},\\
\big(i\gamma^A_{1671}D_{U2A}\Psi_{U2}\big)^2={}&\big(m_{U2}-2G^S_{U2}\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\big)\psi^2_{U2}\\
&-G^P_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}\,\psi^{1*}_{U2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}$$ where the superscripts 1 and 2 on the left select the member. The first term on the right is a mass that depends on the local scalar density; the second couples each member to the conjugate of the other, through the pair field. Both sides transform alike: $\Phi^+_{U2}\tilde\Psi_{U2}$ has the $U(1)_{U2}$ charge $1-y_{U2}=y_{U2}$. The equation for $\bar\Psi_{U2}$ is the Hermitian conjugate of Eq. <a href="#eq:EL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:EL">[eq:EL]</a>. As operator equations, the products on the right need an ordering and a regularization; in this paper they are used in the mean-field approximation of Sec. VI, where the products are replaced by expectation values.

The gauge fields enter $\mathcal L_{U2}$ through $\bar\Psi_{U2}i\gamma^A_{1671}(i\mathcal W_{U2A})\Psi_{U2}=-g'_{U2}y_{U2}B_{U2A}J^A_{U2}
-g_{U2}\boldsymbol W_{U2A}\cdot\boldsymbol J^A_{U2}$, with the currents $J^A_{U2}=\bar\Psi_{U2}\gamma^A_{1671}\Psi_{U2}$ and $\boldsymbol J^A_{U2}=\bar\Psi_{U2}\gamma^A_{1671}\tfrac12\boldsymbol\tau_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$. Their Euler–Lagrange equations are $$\begin{aligned}
\partial_AB^{AC}_{U2}&=g'_{U2}\,y_{U2}\,J^C_{U2},\\
\partial_A\boldsymbol W^{AC}_{U2}-g_{U2}\,\boldsymbol W_{U2A}\times\boldsymbol W^{AC}_{U2}&=g_{U2}\,\boldsymbol J^C_{U2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}$$ The left side of the second equation is the covariant divergence in the adjoint representation; the term with the cross product comes from the variation of $-\tfrac14\boldsymbol W_{U2AC}\cdot\boldsymbol
W^{AC}_{U2}$ with respect to the $\boldsymbol W_{U2A}$ inside the field strength. Since $\partial_C\partial_AB^{AC}_{U2}=0$, the first equation requires the conservation of $J^C_{U2}$, which follows from Eq. <a href="#eq:EL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:EL">[eq:EL]</a> because every term of $\mathcal L_{U2}$ is invariant under $U(1)_{U2}$; $J^4_{U2}=\Psi^\dagger_{U2}\Psi_{U2}$ is the number density of the fermions.

### C. Hamiltonian, number, and the neutralizing background

The Hamiltonian $H_{U2}$ follows from $\mathcal L_{U2}$ in the usual way. With the gauge fields at zero (their mean values in the states of this paper, Sec. VI) it is $$\begin{aligned}
H_{U2}=\int d^7x\,\Big[&\Psi^\dagger_{U2}\big(-i\alpha^j_{1671}\partial_j+\beta_{1671}m_{U2}\big)\Psi_{U2}\\
&-G^S_{U2}\big(\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\big)^2-G^P_{U2}\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}\Big],
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}$$ integrated over the seven space-like coordinates at a fixed time $x4$. The number operator $N_{U2}=\int d^7x\,J^4_{U2}$ is written in the charge-symmetric order, $\tfrac12(\Psi^\dagger_{U2}\Psi_{U2}-\Psi^T_{U2}\Psi^{*}_{U2})$ for the density, so that the vacuum of free fields has no charge; the same order is used for every fermion bilinear in $H_{U2}$. At zero temperature the ground state at the chemical potential $\mu_{U2}$ minimizes $\langle H_{U2}-\mu_{U2}N_{U2}\rangle$.

A state with $\mu_{U2}\neq0$ has a nonzero density of the gauged charge $y_{U2}J^4_{U2}$. By the first of Eqs. <a href="#eq:gaugeeq" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gaugeeq">[eq:gaugeeq]</a> a uniform charge density would produce a field whose energy grows without bound with the volume. As in the description of the electrons of a metal, we assume a uniform background of the opposite $U(1)_{U2}$ charge, so that the mean value of $B_{U2A}$ vanishes; the background has no other effect. In the vacuum, $\mu_{U2}=0$, the density vanishes and no background is needed.

## VI. Mean field: the superfluid state and its spinor solutions

### A. The mean-field approximation

Two exact identities split each quartic term into a part linear in a bilinear and the square of its fluctuation: $$\begin{aligned}
G^S_{U2}(\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2})^2={}&2G^S_{U2}\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle\,\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\\
&-G^S_{U2}\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle^2\\
&+G^S_{U2}\big(\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}-\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle\big)^2,\\
G^P_{U2}\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}={}&G^P_{U2}\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle^*\Phi^+_{U2}
+G^P_{U2}\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\\
&-G^P_{U2}|\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle|^2\\
&+G^P_{U2}\big(\Phi^+_{U2}-\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\big)^\dagger\\
&\quad\times\big(\Phi^+_{U2}-\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}$$ where $\langle\ \rangle$ is the expectation value in the ground state. The mean-field approximation drops the squares of the fluctuations (the last term of each line). With the *effective mass* and the *pair field* $$M_{U2}=m_{U2}-2G^S_{U2}\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle,\qquad
\Delta_{U2}=G^P_{U2}\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle,
\tag{25}$$ and the gauge fields at their mean values, zero (the condensate is an $SU(2)_{U2}$ singlet, and the $U(1)_{U2}$ charge is neutralized, Sec. V), the Lagrangian becomes $$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L^{\rm MF}_{U2}={}&\bar\Psi_{U2}\big(i\gamma^A_{1671}\partial_A-M_{U2}\big)\Psi_{U2}
+\Delta^*_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}+\Delta_{U2}\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}\\
&-\frac{|\Delta_{U2}|^2}{G^P_{U2}}-\frac{(M_{U2}-m_{U2})^2}{4G^S_{U2}},
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}$$ (the constants follow from $\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle=(m_{U2}-M_{U2})/(2G^S_{U2})$ and $\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle=\Delta_{U2}/G^P_{U2}$), and Eq. <a href="#eq:EL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:EL">[eq:EL]</a> becomes the mean-field Dirac equation $$i\gamma^A_{1671}\partial_A\Psi_{U2}=M_{U2}\Psi_{U2}+\Delta_{U2}\tilde\Psi_{U2}.
\tag{27}$$ The same equations are the saddle point of the path integral after a Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation ([Hubbard 1959](#ref-Hubbard1959); [Stratonovich 1958](#ref-Stratonovich1957)), which introduces $M_{U2}$ and $\Delta_{U2}$ as auxiliary fields; the mean-field approximation is the leading order of that formulation, as in the theories of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer ([Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer 1957](#ref-BCS1957)) and of Nambu and Jona-Lasinio ([Y. Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961](#ref-NJL1961)). The fluctuations of the auxiliary fields about the saddle point are the collective modes of Sec. VIII.

### B. The Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix

In a large periodic box of volume $\mathrm{Vol}_{U2}$ the Fourier components of the fields at a fixed time are $\psi^1_{U2}(\vec q)=\mathrm{Vol}_{U2}^{-1/2}\int d^7x\,\exp(-i\vec q\cdot\vec x)\,
\psi^1_{U2}$, and the same for $\psi^2_{U2}$. The Hamiltonian of Eq. <a href="#eq:LMF" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:LMF">[eq:LMF]</a>, minus $\mu_{U2}N_{U2}$, couples $\psi^1_{U2}$ at the momentum $\vec q$ only with $\psi^2_{U2}$ at $-\vec q$. With the 32-component *Nambu spinor* $\mathcal N_{U2}(\vec q)=\big(\psi^1_{U2}(\vec q),\ \psi^2_{U2}(-\vec q)^{\dagger T}\big)$, whose lower half holds the Hermitian conjugates of the components of $\psi^2_{U2}(-\vec q)$, $$\begin{aligned}
H^{\rm MF}_{U2}-\mu_{U2}N_{U2}={}&\sum_{\vec q}\mathcal N^\dagger_{U2}\mathsf H_{U2}\mathcal N_{U2}\\
&+\mathrm{Vol}_{U2}\Big[\frac{|\Delta_{U2}|^2}{G^P_{U2}}+\frac{(M_{U2}-m_{U2})^2}{4G^S_{U2}}\Big],
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}$$ with the sum over the momenta of the box and the $32\times32$ Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix $$\begin{aligned}
\mathsf H_{U2}(\vec q)&=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf h_{U2}-\mu_{U2} & \Delta_{U2}C^+_{1671}\\
\Delta^*_{U2}C^+_{1671} & \mu_{U2}-\beta_{1671}\mathsf h_{U2}\beta_{1671}\end{pmatrix},\\
\mathsf h_{U2}&=\alpha^j_{1671}q^j+\beta_{1671}M_{U2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}$$ where $\mu_{U2}$ in a block stands for $\mu_{U2}\mathsf 1_{16}$, and $-\beta_{1671}\mathsf h_{U2}\beta_{1671}=\alpha^j_{1671}q^j-\beta_{1671}M_{U2}$. The upper left block is the Dirac Hamiltonian of $\psi^1_{U2}$ in momentum space, minus the chemical potential. The lower right block comes from the same terms for $\psi^2_{U2}(-\vec q)$: written with the Hermitian-conjugate components, the bilinear of $\psi^2_{U2}$ with a $16\times16$ matrix turns into minus the bilinear of the transposed matrix (one anticommutation per term), and $(\mathsf h_{U2}(-\vec q)-\mu_{U2}\mathsf 1_{16})^T=-\alpha^j_{1671}q^j+\beta_{1671}M_{U2}-\mu_{U2}\mathsf 1_{16}$ because $\alpha^j_{1671}$ and $\beta_{1671}$ are symmetric; the constant that the anticommutation produces cancels in the charge-symmetric order, and $\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathsf H_{U2}=0$. The off-diagonal blocks come from $\Delta^*_{U2}\Phi^+_{U2}+\Delta_{U2}\Phi^{+\dagger}_{U2}$. The Heisenberg equation of motion, $i\partial_4\mathcal N_{U2}=\mathsf H_{U2}\mathcal N_{U2}$, is Eq. <a href="#eq:MFDirac" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:MFDirac">[eq:MFDirac]</a> in Hamiltonian form (multiply Eq. <a href="#eq:MFDirac" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:MFDirac">[eq:MFDirac]</a> by $\beta_{1671}$). The spectrum does not depend on the phase of $\Delta_{U2}$ (R3-U1): the phase is removed by multiplying $\psi^1_{U2}$ and $\psi^2_{U2}$ by the same constant phase. Below $\Delta_{U2}$ is real and positive unless said otherwise.

### C. Spectrum and spinor solutions

*Invariant planes.* Let $\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}$ be a real normalized eigenvector of $\mathsf h_{U2}(\vec q)$ with the eigenvalue $\pm\varepsilon_{U2}$, $\varepsilon_{U2}=(q^2+M^2_{U2})^{1/2}$; there are eight of each sign, because $\mathsf h_{U2}^2=\varepsilon_{U2}^2\mathsf 1_{16}$ and $\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathsf h_{U2}=0$. From $\beta_{1671}\alpha^j_{1671}=-\alpha^j_{1671}\beta_{1671}$ it follows that $(\alpha^j_{1671}q^j-\beta_{1671}M_{U2})\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}=
-\beta_{1671}\mathsf h_{U2}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}=\mp\varepsilon_{U2}\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}$, and $C^+_{1671}=\beta_{1671}$ maps $\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}$ to $\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}$ and back. So the plane spanned by $(\mathsf e^\pm_{U2},0)$ and $(0,\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2})$ is invariant, and on it $\mathsf H_{U2}$ is the $2\times2$ matrix $$\begin{pmatrix}\xi^\pm_{U2}&\Delta_{U2}\\ \Delta_{U2}&-\xi^\pm_{U2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\xi^\pm_{U2}=\pm\varepsilon_{U2}-\mu_{U2},
\tag{30}$$ with the eigenvalues $\pm[(\xi^\pm_{U2})^2+\Delta_{U2}^2]^{1/2}$. The 16 planes (one for each eigenvector of $\mathsf h_{U2}$) are mutually orthogonal and fill the 32 dimensions. The eigenvalues of $\mathsf H_{U2}$ are therefore $\pm E^+_{U2}$ and $\pm E^-_{U2}$, each eightfold, $$E^\pm_{U2}=\big[(\varepsilon_{U2}\pm\mu_{U2})^2+\Delta_{U2}^2\big]^{1/2}
\tag{31}$$ (R3-C1). The planes of the positive-energy eigenvectors $\mathsf e^+_{U2}$ (particles) carry $E^-_{U2}$, those of $\mathsf e^-_{U2}$ (antiparticles) carry $E^+_{U2}$. The eigenvectors, the spinor solutions of the mean-field equations, are explicit (R3-C3): $$\begin{aligned}
+E^\mp_{U2}:&\quad \big(u^\pm_{U2}\,\mathsf e^\pm_{U2},\ v^\pm_{U2}\,\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}\big),\\
-E^\mp_{U2}:&\quad \big(-v^\pm_{U2}\,\mathsf e^\pm_{U2},\ u^\pm_{U2}\,\beta_{1671}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}\big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}$$ with the Bogoliubov coefficients $u^\pm_{U2}=[(1+\xi^\pm_{U2}/E^\mp_{U2})/2]^{1/2}$ and $v^\pm_{U2}=[(1-\xi^\pm_{U2}/E^\mp_{U2})/2]^{1/2}$, which satisfy $(u^\pm_{U2})^2+(v^\pm_{U2})^2=1$ and $2u^\pm_{U2}v^\pm_{U2}=\Delta_{U2}/E^\mp_{U2}$. As functions of space and time, $\mathcal N_{U2}\propto(u^\pm_{U2}\mathsf e^\pm_{U2},v^\pm_{U2}\beta_{1671}\mathsf
e^\pm_{U2})\exp(i\vec q\cdot\vec x-iE^\mp_{U2}x4)$ and its partner of negative energy solve the self-interacting Dirac equation <a href="#eq:MFDirac" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:MFDirac">[eq:MFDirac]</a> in Hamiltonian form, for every $\vec q$: these are the spinor solutions that the task asks for. Each of them mixes a particle of $\psi^1_{U2}$ with a hole of $\psi^2_{U2}$ of opposite momentum, with the weights $u^\pm_{U2}$ and $v^\pm_{U2}$.

*The same spectrum from anticommutators.* Write $\mathsf H_{U2}$ as the sum of four real symmetric matrices: the kinetic part $\mathrm{diag}(\alpha^j_{1671}q^j,\alpha^j_{1671}q^j)$, the mass part $M_{U2}\mathsf V^3_{U2}$ with $\mathsf V^3_{U2}=\mathrm{diag}(\beta_{1671},-\beta_{1671})$, the chemical-potential part $-\mu_{U2}\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathsf 1_{16},-\mathsf 1_{16})$, and the pair part $\Delta_{U2}\mathsf V^1_{U2}$ with $\mathsf V^1_{U2}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&C^+_{1671}\\
C^+_{1671}&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)$. The kinetic, the mass and the pair part anticommute with each other (R3-A13); the chemical-potential part commutes with the first two and anticommutes with the third. Hence all cross terms of the square vanish except those of the chemical-potential part with the kinetic and the mass part, and $\mathsf H_{U2}^2=(\varepsilon_{U2}^2+\mu_{U2}^2+\Delta_{U2}^2)\mathsf 1_{32}+2\mathsf Y_{U2}$, where $\mathsf Y_{U2}=-\mu_{U2}\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathsf 1_{16},-\mathsf 1_{16})\,[\mathrm{diag}(\alpha^j_{1671}q^j,\alpha^j_{1671}q^j)+M_{U2}\mathsf V^3_{U2}]$ has the square $\mu^2_{U2}\varepsilon^2_{U2}\mathsf 1_{32}$. The eigenvalues of $\mathsf H^2_{U2}$ are $\varepsilon^2_{U2}+\mu^2_{U2}+\Delta^2_{U2}\pm2\mu_{U2}\varepsilon_{U2}=(E^\pm_{U2})^2$.

*The pseudoscalar pair, for comparison.* With $C^-_{1671}$ in place of $C^+_{1671}$ the pair part *commutes* with the mass part (R3-A13), and the same computation gives $\mathsf
H^2_{U2}=(\varepsilon^2_{U2}+\mu^2_{U2}+\Delta^2_{U2})\mathsf 1_{32}+2\mathsf Y'_{U2}$ with $(\mathsf Y'_{U2})^2=(\mu^2_{U2}\varepsilon^2_{U2}+M^2_{U2}\Delta^2_{U2})\mathsf 1_{32}$, where $\mathsf
Y'_{U2}$ adds the product of the mass and the pair part to $\mathsf Y_{U2}$; hence the eigenvalues $$\pm\big[\varepsilon^2_{U2}+\mu^2_{U2}+\Delta^2_{U2}\pm2(\mu^2_{U2}\varepsilon^2_{U2}+M^2_{U2}\Delta^2_{U2})^{1/2}\big]^{1/2}
\tag{33}$$ (R3-C2). At a Fermi surface the difference matters (R3-C6): for the spin-zero pair the smallest quasi-particle energy is $\Delta_{U2}$, reached at $\varepsilon_{U2}=\mu_{U2}$; for the pseudoscalar pair the minimum of Eq. <a href="#eq:Cminus" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Cminus">[eq:Cminus]</a> is $$\Delta_{U2}\big(1-M^2_{U2}/\mu^2_{U2}\big)^{1/2}=\Delta_{U2}\,q^F_{U2}/\mu_{U2},
\tag{34}$$ ($q^F_{U2}=(\mu^2_{U2}-M^2_{U2})^{1/2}$ the Fermi momentum), which vanishes in the non-relativistic limit $q^F_{U2}\ll M_{U2}$. The pseudoscalar pair cannot open a gap at a slow Fermi surface, in accord with Eq. <a href="#eq:restframe" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:restframe">[eq:restframe]</a>; the spin-zero pair opens the full gap. ### D. The superfluid ground state and the gap equation

In each invariant plane the occupied state is the eigenvector of negative eigenvalue. For an eigenvector $\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}$ of $\mathsf h_{U2}(\vec q)$ define the mode annihilators $a_{U2}=(\mathsf e^\pm_{U2})^T\psi^1_{U2}(\vec q)$ and $d_{U2}=(\beta_{1671}\mathsf
e^\pm_{U2})^T\psi^2_{U2}(-\vec q)$, and the pair creator $b^\dagger_{U2}=a^\dagger_{U2}d^\dagger_{U2}$; in the plane the Hamiltonian is $(a^\dagger_{U2},d_{U2})$ times the matrix <a href="#eq:block" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:block">[eq:block]</a> times $(a_{U2},d^\dagger_{U2})^T$. The ground state is $$|\mathrm{SF}_{U2}\rangle=\prod_{\vec q}\ \prod_{\mathsf e^\pm_{U2}}\big(u^\pm_{U2}-v^\pm_{U2}\,b^\dagger_{U2}\big)\,|\varnothing_{U2}\rangle,
\tag{35}$$ the product over the momenta of the box and over the 16 eigenvectors of each $\mathsf
h_{U2}(\vec q)$, applied to the state $|\varnothing_{U2}\rangle$ without any fermion in any mode. One checks that the quasi-particle annihilators $u^\pm_{U2}a_{U2}+v^\pm_{U2}d^\dagger_{U2}$ and $-v^\pm_{U2}a^\dagger_{U2}+u^\pm_{U2}d_{U2}$ annihilate each factor. In the factors of the antiparticle branch $\xi^-_{U2}=-\varepsilon_{U2}-\mu_{U2}$ is large and negative, $v^-_{U2}\approx1$: the negative-energy states of both members are filled (the Dirac sea; $\beta_{1671}\mathsf
e^-_{U2}$ is a negative-energy eigenvector of $\mathsf h_{U2}(-\vec q)$). In the factors of the particle branch $v^+_{U2}\approx1$ below and $\approx0$ above the Fermi surface, and the superposition is spread over a width $\Delta_{U2}$ around it: $|\mathrm{SF}_{U2}\rangle$ is a coherent state of spin-zero pairs, each pair a member $\psi^1_{U2}$ at $\vec q$ and a member $\psi^2_{U2}$ at $-\vec q$ in the state of total spin zero. It is the analogue, for the $7{+}1$ spacetime and a Dirac doublet, of the state of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer.

In this state (R3-C3 with the Hellmann–Feynman theorem, which here is exact because the state is the ground state of Eq. <a href="#eq:HMF" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:HMF">[eq:HMF]</a>) $$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle}{\Delta_{U2}}&=4\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\Big(\frac1{E^+_{U2}}+\frac1{E^-_{U2}}\Big),\\
\langle\bar\Psi_{U2}\Psi_{U2}\rangle&=-8M_{U2}\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{U2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{U2}+\mu_{U2}}{E^+_{U2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{U2}-\mu_{U2}}{E^-_{U2}}\Big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}$$ per unit volume, where $\sum_{\vec q}\to\mathrm{Vol}_{U2}\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7$. The first follows from $\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\,\mathrm{Vol}_{U2}=\sum u^\pm_{U2}v^\pm_{U2}$ over all planes, the second from the derivative of the energy of the occupied states with respect to $M_{U2}$. Inserted in Eq. <a href="#eq:MD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:MD">[eq:MD]</a> they give the *gap equation* and the *mass equation*, $$\begin{aligned}
\frac1{G^P_{U2}}&=4\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\Big(\frac1{E^+_{U2}}+\frac1{E^-_{U2}}\Big),\\
M_{U2}-m_{U2}&=16\,G^S_{U2}M_{U2}\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\,\frac1{\varepsilon_{U2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{U2}+\mu_{U2}}{E^+_{U2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{U2}-\mu_{U2}}{E^-_{U2}}\Big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}$$ the first for $\Delta_{U2}\neq0$. The spinors <a href="#eq:spinors" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:spinors">[eq:spinors]</a> with $M_{U2}$ and $\Delta_{U2}$ that solve Eqs. <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a> are the self-consistent solutions of the self-interacting Dirac equation <a href="#eq:EL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:EL">[eq:EL]</a> in mean field. The next section shows that Eqs. <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a> are the conditions for a stationary point of the effective potential, and decides which solution is the ground state.

## VII. The effective potential of the superfluid and its minima

### A. Definition

The *effective potential* $\Omega_{U2}(M_{U2},\Delta_{U2})$ is the expectation value of $H^{\rm MF}_{U2}-\mu_{U2}N_{U2}$ per unit volume in the ground state of Eq. <a href="#eq:HMF" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:HMF">[eq:HMF]</a> at given $M_{U2}$ and $\Delta_{U2}$, at zero temperature. That ground state fills, for every $\vec q$, the 16 negative eigenvalues of $\mathsf H_{U2}$, eight equal to $-E^+_{U2}$ and eight to $-E^-_{U2}$; with no constant from the charge-symmetric order, $$\Omega_{U2}=\frac{(M_{U2}-m_{U2})^2}{4G^S_{U2}}+\frac{|\Delta_{U2}|^2}{G^P_{U2}}
-8\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\big(E^+_{U2}+E^-_{U2}\big).
\tag{38}$$ The first two terms form the classical part $\Omega^{\rm cl}_{U2}$; the integral is the energy of the filled states, measured from the chemical potential. Since $\partial E^\pm_{U2}/\partial\Delta^*_{U2}=\Delta_{U2}/(2E^\pm_{U2})$ and $\partial E^\pm_{U2}/\partial M_{U2}=(\varepsilon_{U2}\pm\mu_{U2})M_{U2}/(\varepsilon_{U2}E^\pm_{U2})$, the conditions $\partial\Omega_{U2}/\partial\Delta^*_{U2}=0$ and $\partial\Omega_{U2}/\partial
M_{U2}=0$ are exactly the gap and the mass equations <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a>: *the stationary points of the effective potential are the self-consistent solutions of the self-interacting Dirac equation*, and the ground state is the global minimum. $\Omega_{U2}$ depends on $\Delta_{U2}$ only through $|\Delta_{U2}|$ (R3-U1): a minimum at $\Delta_{U2}\neq0$ is a whole circle of minima in the complex plane of $\Delta_{U2}$, the Mexican hat, and the choice of one point on it breaks $U(1)_{U2}$.

The integral diverges; the theory needs a cutoff $\Lambda_{U2}$ (Sec. IX), and all numbers below are in units $\Lambda_{U2}=1$. We use two regularizations. **(R7)**: the length $q$ of the seven-dimensional momentum is cut at $\Lambda_{U2}$; for an integrand that depends on $q$ only, $\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7(\dots)=a^{(7)}_{U2}\int_0^{\Lambda_{U2}}q^6(\dots)\,dq$ with $a^{(7)}_{U2}=A_7/(2\pi)^7=1/(120\pi^4)$, where $A_7=16\pi^3/15$ is the area of the unit sphere in seven dimensions. (R7) is used at every chemical potential. **(R8)**: in the vacuum the fermion term is Lorentz invariant. The integral over the imaginary-time frequency $\kappa^{(4)}_{U2}$ (the eighth component of the Euclidean momentum) of $\ln[(\kappa^{(4)}_{U2})^2+(E^+_{U2})^2]/(2\pi)$ equals $E^+_{U2}$ up to a constant that does not depend on $E^+_{U2}$; with it, $-16\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7(q^2+s_{U2})^{1/2}$ becomes $-16\int d^8\kappa_{U2}/(2\pi)^8\ln(\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2})$, with $s_{U2}=M^2_{U2}+|\Delta_{U2}|^2$ and $\kappa_{U2}$ the length of the Euclidean 8-momentum. Cutting $\kappa_{U2}$ at $\Lambda_{U2}$, and with $a^{(8)}_{U2}=A_8/(2\pi)^8=1/(768\pi^4)$, $A_8=\pi^4/3$, $$\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{U2}^{\rm (R8)}={}&\Omega^{\rm cl}_{U2}-8a^{(8)}_{U2}L_{U2}(s_{U2}),\\
L_{U2}(s_{U2})={}&2\int_0^1\kappa_{U2}^7\ln(\kappa_{U2}^2+s_{U2})\,d\kappa_{U2}\\
={}&\tfrac14(1-s_{U2}^4)\ln(1+s_{U2})+\tfrac14s_{U2}^4\ln s_{U2}\\
&-\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{12}s_{U2}-\tfrac18s_{U2}^2+\tfrac14s_{U2}^3,
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}$$ with the derivative $L'_{U2}(s_{U2})=\tfrac13-\tfrac12s_{U2}+s_{U2}^2-s_{U2}^3\ln[(1+s_{U2})/s_{U2}]$ (R3-P2; the closed form follows by one integration by parts). (R8) respects the Lorentz invariance of the vacuum; (R7) does not, and the comparison of the two shows which results depend on the regularization. In (R7) the vacuum integrals are elementary too: with $\mathcal J^{(\iota)}_{U2}(s_{U2})=\int_0^1q^\iota(q^2+s_{U2})^{-1/2}dq$ for $\iota=0,2,\dots,8$, $\mathcal J^{(0)}_{U2}=\ln[(1+(1+s_{U2})^{1/2})/s^{1/2}_{U2}]$, $\iota\,\mathcal J^{(\iota)}_{U2}=(1+s_{U2})^{1/2}-(\iota-1)s_{U2}\mathcal J^{(\iota-2)}_{U2}$, and $\int_0^1q^6(q^2+s_{U2})^{1/2}dq=\mathcal J^{(8)}_{U2}+s_{U2}\mathcal J^{(6)}_{U2}$ (R3-P1). In the programs $\Omega_{U2}$ is computed relative to the normal state of mass $m_{U2}$ at the same $\mu_{U2}$; this subtracts a constant and leaves the minima unchanged.

### B. The vacuum: the Mexican hat

At $\mu_{U2}=0$, $E^+_{U2}=E^-_{U2}=(q^2+s_{U2})^{1/2}$, and the gap equation <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a> reads $1/G^P_{U2}=8\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7(q^2+s_{U2})^{-1/2}$. Its right side decreases as $s_{U2}$ grows, so it has a root with $\Delta_{U2}\neq0$ only if $G^P_{U2}$ exceeds the value at $s_{U2}\to0$, the *critical coupling* $$\begin{aligned}
G^{\rm crit}_{U2}&=\frac{3}{4a^{(7)}_{U2}}=90\pi^4\Lambda_{U2}^{-6}&&\text{(R7)},\\
G^{\rm crit}_{U2}&=\frac{3}{8a^{(8)}_{U2}}=288\pi^4\Lambda_{U2}^{-6}&&\text{(R8)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}$$ (R3-P3: $8a^{(7)}_{U2}\int_0^1q^5dq=4a^{(7)}_{U2}/3$, and $8a^{(8)}_{U2}L'_{U2}(0)=8a^{(8)}_{U2}/3$). We measure the couplings by $\lambda^P_{U2}=G^P_{U2}/G^{\rm crit}_{U2}$ and $\lambda^S_{U2}=4G^S_{U2}/
G^{\rm crit}_{U2}$; the factor 4 makes $\lambda^S_{U2}=1$ the critical value of the mass equation, which at $m_{U2}=0$ reads $1/(4G^S_{U2})=8\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7(q^2+s_{U2})^{-1/2}$.

With $m_{U2}=0$ and without the scalar coupling ($M_{U2}=0$): for $\lambda^P_{U2}<1$ the minimum is $\Delta_{U2}=0$ in both regularizations (R3-P5); for $\lambda^P_{U2}>1$ the minimum is at $\Delta_{U2}\neq0$ and $\Delta_{U2}=0$ is a local maximum (R3-P4, 102 values of $\lambda^P_{U2}$ up to 3). Near the critical coupling the expansion of Eq. <a href="#eq:Omega" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Omega">[eq:Omega]</a> in $\Delta_{U2}$ gives the Landau form of the Mexican hat, $$\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{U2}(\Delta_{U2})-\Omega_{U2}(0)={}&\tfrac43a^{(7)}_{U2}\Big(\frac1{\lambda^P_{U2}}-1\Big)|\Delta_{U2}|^2\\
&+\tfrac12a^{(7)}_{U2}|\Delta_{U2}|^4+\dots\quad\text{(R7)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}$$ from $(q^2+|\Delta_{U2}|^2)^{1/2}=q+|\Delta_{U2}|^2/(2q)-|\Delta_{U2}|^4/(8q^3)+\dots$ under $\int_0^1q^6dq$ (the expansion fails only at $q\lesssim|\Delta_{U2}|$, which changes the next order, $|\Delta_{U2}|^6\ln|\Delta_{U2}|$). The minimum is at $$\begin{aligned}
|\Delta_{U2}|^2&=\tfrac43\big(1-1/\lambda^P_{U2}\big)&&\text{(R7)},\\
|\Delta_{U2}|^2&=\tfrac23\big(1-1/\lambda^P_{U2}\big)&&\text{(R8)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}$$ the second from $L_{U2}(s_{U2})=L_{U2}(0)+s_{U2}/3-s_{U2}^2/4+\dots$ (R3-P6: ratio of the computed gap to these forms at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.0005$ within 1 per cent). Figure 2 shows the hat, Fig. 3 and Table II the gap: at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.05$, $\Delta_{U2}=0.26370$ (R7) and $0.18375$ (R8). The gap is a sizeable fraction of the cutoff once $\lambda^P_{U2}$ exceeds 1 by a few per cent, and the two regularizations differ by the factor $1.44$: in the vacuum the cutoff sets the scale.

### C. Mass against pair in the vacuum

With $m_{U2}=0$ and both couplings, Eq. <a href="#eq:Omega" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Omega">[eq:Omega]</a> in the vacuum reads $$\Omega_{U2}=\frac{1}{G^{\rm crit}_{U2}}\Big(\frac{M^2_{U2}}{\lambda^S_{U2}}+\frac{|\Delta_{U2}|^2}{\lambda^P_{U2}}\Big)
-16\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}(q^2+s_{U2})^{1/2}.
\tag{43}$$ The fermion term depends on $s_{U2}=M^2_{U2}+|\Delta_{U2}|^2$ only. The reason is algebraic: the vertices $\mathsf V^1_{U2}$ and $\mathsf V^3_{U2}$ of $\Delta_1$ (the real part of $\Delta_{U2}$) and of $M_{U2}$ anticommute with each other and with the kinetic part (R3-A13), and so does the vertex $\mathsf V^2_{U2}=i\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathsf 1_{16},-\mathsf 1_{16})\mathsf V^1_{U2}$ of $\Delta_2$ (the imaginary part); at $\mu_{U2}=0$ the square of $\mathsf H_{U2}$ is then $(q^2+M^2_{U2}+\Delta_1^2+\Delta_2^2)\mathsf 1_{32}$. At fixed $s_{U2}$ the classical part is smallest when all of $s_{U2}$ is put into the field with the larger coupling. Hence (R3-P7, Fig. 4): *the vacuum is a superfluid, and $U(2)_{U2}$ is broken, if and only if $\lambda^P_{U2}>\max(1,\lambda^S_{U2})$*. For $\lambda^S_{U2}>\max(1,\lambda^P_{U2})$ the vacuum is the massive state $(M_{U2},0)$, which breaks no gauge symmetry, and for $\lambda^P_{U2}=\lambda^S_{U2}>1$ the minima form a whole circle $M^2_{U2}+|\Delta_{U2}|^2=$ const. That degeneracy is the trace of a larger symmetry of the massless free doublet, which rotates the mass into the pair field; the classical part breaks it unless $G^P_{U2}=4G^S_{U2}$.

### D. Finite density: every attraction pairs

For $\mu_{U2}>M_{U2}$ the particle branch has a Fermi surface at $q=q^F_{U2}$, where $E^-_{U2}=|\Delta_{U2}|$. Near it $dq=(\varepsilon_{U2}/q)\,d\xi^+_{U2}$, and $$\begin{aligned}
4\int\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\frac1{E^-_{U2}}&\simeq4a^{(7)}_{U2}(q^F_{U2})^6\frac{\mu_{U2}}{q^F_{U2}}\\
&\quad\times\int\frac{d\xi^+_{U2}}{[(\xi^+_{U2})^2+|\Delta_{U2}|^2]^{1/2}}\\
&\simeq8a^{(7)}_{U2}(q^F_{U2})^5\mu_{U2}\ln\frac1{|\Delta_{U2}|}
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}$$ as $\Delta_{U2}\to0$ (R3-P8a, computed at $\Delta_{U2}=10^{-100}$ and $10^{-200}$). The right side of the gap equation therefore grows without bound as $\Delta_{U2}\to0$, and the gap equation has a root for *every* $G^P_{U2}>0$: the Cooper instability ([Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer 1957](#ref-BCS1957)). In the weak-coupling limit $$\ln|\Delta_{U2}|=\text{const}-\frac{1}{8a^{(7)}_{U2}(q^F_{U2})^5\mu_{U2}\,G^P_{U2}},
\tag{45}$$ the law of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer in seven space dimensions: at $\mu_{U2}=0.4$ the slope of the computed $\ln\Delta_{U2}$ against $1/G^P_{U2}$ agrees with Eq. <a href="#eq:bcs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bcs">[eq:bcs]</a>, ratio $1.00000$ (R3-P9). At every $\mu_{U2}>0$ of a scan of 60 values and at every coupling tested, $\lambda^P_{U2}\in\{0.5,0.8,0.95,1.05\}$, the minimum has $\Delta_{U2}>0$ (R3-P8); where the gap is below $10^{-300}$, the smallest positive number of the arithmetic, its logarithm follows from Eq. <a href="#eq:cooper" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cooper">[eq:cooper]</a>. Figure 5 shows both results, and Fig. 6 the gap on the plane of the coupling and the chemical potential. The number density $n_{U2}=-\partial\Omega_{U2}/\partial\mu_{U2}=8\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7[(\varepsilon_{U2}+\mu_{U2})/E^+_{U2}
-(\varepsilon_{U2}-\mu_{U2})/E^-_{U2}]$ of the superfluid agrees with the derivative of $\Omega^{\min}_{U2}$ computed by differences (R3-P11); no derivative of $\Delta_{U2}$ is needed, because $\Omega_{U2}$ is stationary in $\Delta_{U2}$.

### E. Breaking of the gauge group driven by density

If the scalar coupling is the stronger one, $\lambda^S_{U2}>\lambda^P_{U2}>1$, the vacuum is the massive state, and $U(2)_{U2}$ is unbroken. For $\lambda^S_{U2}=1.1$ and $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.05$ its mass is $M_{U2}=0.379601$. While $\mu_{U2}<M_{U2}$ there are no states to fill, and the potential of the massive state does not change with $\mu_{U2}$; the superfluid, in contrast, lowers its potential as $\mu_{U2}$ grows. At $\mu_{U2}\approx0.19500$ the global minimum jumps from the massive symmetric state to the superfluid with $M_{U2}=0$ (R3-P10, Fig. 7); at $\mu_{U2}=0.6$ the pair field is $0.56061$. The transition is of first order: $M_{U2}$ and $\Delta_{U2}$ jump. The breaking $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$ is then driven by the density, not by the strength of the coupling.

### F. The chiral doublet

A doublet of left-handed fields has no mass term (Sec. III) and cannot carry the gauged $U(1)_{U2}$ (Sec. IV), but it can pair. Its Nambu spinor has 16 components, the left-handed $\psi^1_{U2}$ and the conjugate of the left-handed $\psi^2_{U2}$, which is right-handed; with $C^+_{1671}$ the eigenvalues are $\pm[(q\mp\mu_{U2})^2+|\Delta_{U2}|^2]^{1/2}$, each fourfold (R3-C5): half the states of the Dirac doublet. Its potential at the coupling $G^P_{U2}$ is half that of the Dirac doublet at $G^P_{U2}/2$, so in the vacuum it pairs only for $G^P_{U2}>2G^{\rm crit}_{U2}$ ($180\pi^4\Lambda_{U2}^{-6}$ in R7), while at finite density it pairs for any attraction (R3-P12, with a nonzero gap at $\lambda^P_{U2}=0.8$, $\mu_{U2}=0.4$).

## VIII. The breaking of the gauge group and the bosons it produces

### A. What “broken” means for a gauge group

At a minimum with $\Delta_{U2}\neq0$ the pair field is invariant under $SU(2)_{U2}$ and changes its phase under $U(1)_{U2}$ \[Eq. <a href="#eq:detE" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:detE">[eq:detE]</a>\]. The transformations that leave it invariant are those with $\det\Upsilon_{U2}=1$, so the condensate breaks $$U(2)_{U2}\ \to\ SU(2)_{U2},
\tag{46}$$ with one broken generator, the phase $\theta_{U2}$ of $\Delta_{U2}=|\Delta_{U2}|\exp(i\theta_{U2})$. A local symmetry cannot be broken spontaneously in the literal sense (Elitzur’s theorem ([Elitzur 1975](#ref-Elitzur1975))): without a choice of gauge, $\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle$ averages to zero over the gauge orbit. The statement “the gauge group is spontaneously broken” means, as for superconductors and for the electroweak theory ([Anderson 1963](#ref-Anderson1963); [Englert and Brout 1964](#ref-EnglertBrout1964); [Higgs 1964](#ref-Higgs1964); [Guralnik, Hagen, and Kibble 1964](#ref-GHK1964)): in a fixed gauge $\langle\Phi^+_{U2}\rangle\neq0$; gauge-invariantly, the gauge boson of the broken generator is massive (the Meissner effect, Sec. VIII D), the would-be Goldstone boson is absorbed by it, and the amplitude $|\Delta_{U2}|$ of the condensate is nonzero. The rest of this section derives these statements and the new scalar.

### B. Small oscillations in the random-phase approximation

Let the composite fields oscillate about the minimum, $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,M_{U2})\to(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,M_{U2})_{\min}+(\text{amplitudes})\times\exp(-iw_{U2}x4)$, where $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ are the real and imaginary parts of $\Delta_{U2}$, and the minimum is taken with $\Delta_2=0$. The Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix depends on them linearly, through the vertices $\mathsf V^1_{U2}$, $\mathsf V^2_{U2}=i\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathsf 1_{16},-\mathsf 1_{16})\mathsf V^1_{U2}$ and $\mathsf V^3_{U2}$ of Sec. VI C. The linearized self-consistency conditions have a solution with nonzero amplitudes if and only if $\det\mathsf R_{U2}(w_{U2})=0$, where $$\mathsf R_{U2}(w_{U2})=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\frac2{G^P_{U2}},\frac2{G^P_{U2}},\frac1{2G^S_{U2}}\Big)+\Pi_{U2}(w_{U2})
\tag{47}$$ is the $3\times3$ matrix of the second derivatives of the classical part $\Omega^{\rm cl}_{U2}$ with respect to $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,M_{U2})$ plus the polarization $\Pi_{U2}$ of the fermions (the random-phase approximation). Its entry in the first row and column is $$\begin{aligned}
\big[\Pi_{U2}(w_{U2})\big]_{11}={}&\int\!\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\\
&\times\sum_{\rm o,e}\big|{}_{U2}\langle\mathrm e|\mathsf V^1_{U2}|\mathrm o\rangle_{U2}\big|^2\\
&\times\Big[\frac{1}{w_{U2}-\mathcal E^{\rm e}_{U2}+\mathcal E^{\rm o}_{U2}}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{w_{U2}+\mathcal E^{\rm e}_{U2}-\mathcal E^{\rm o}_{U2}}\Big],
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}$$ summed over the occupied eigenvectors $|\mathrm o\rangle_{U2}$ (eigenvalue $\mathcal E^{\rm o}_{U2}<0$) and the empty eigenvectors $|\mathrm e\rangle_{U2}$ (eigenvalue $\mathcal E^{\rm e}_{U2}\ge0$) of $\mathsf H_{U2}(\vec q)$. The other entries follow by replacing, in the first term, the vertex of the bra-ket on the left by that of the row and the one on the right by that of the column, and in the second term the reverse. At $w_{U2}=0$, Eq. <a href="#eq:Pi" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Pi">[eq:Pi]</a> is second-order perturbation theory for the energy of the occupied states, so $\mathsf R_{U2}(0)$ is the Hessian of $\Omega_{U2}$ at the minimum (R3-M3). For $G^S_{U2}=0$ the mass is frozen at $m_{U2}$ and $\mathsf R_{U2}$ is the $2\times2$ matrix of $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$.

*The Goldstone mode.* Because $\Omega_{U2}$ depends on $|\Delta_{U2}|$ only, the second derivative in the direction of the phase vanishes at the minimum: $[\mathsf R_{U2}(0)]_{22}=2/G^P_{U2}
+[\Pi_{U2}(0)]_{22}=0$, which is the gap equation in another form. The programs verify it at every minimum computed, in the vacuum with and without a bare mass and at finite density (R3-M2, R3-M8, R3-M9), to the accuracy of the quadrature: the oscillation of the phase has zero frequency at zero momentum, the Goldstone mode ([Yoichiro Nambu 1960](#ref-Nambu1960); [Goldstone 1961](#ref-Goldstone1961)).

### C. The amplitude mode $\sigma_{U2}$: a new neutral scalar

In the vacuum without scalar coupling the amplitude entry can be evaluated in closed form. At $\mu_{U2}=0$ the eigenvalues of $\mathsf H_{U2}$ are $\pm E^+_{U2}$ with $E^+_{U2}=(q^2+s_{U2})^{1/2}$, and the projectors on the empty and the occupied eigenvectors are $\tfrac12(\mathsf 1_{32}\pm\mathsf H_{U2}/E^+_{U2})$. With $\mathsf H_{U2}=\mathsf H_{U2}|_{\Delta_1=0}+\Delta_1\mathsf V^1_{U2}$, where the first part anticommutes with $\mathsf V^1_{U2}$ and has the square $(q^2+M^2_{U2})\mathsf 1_{32}$, and with $(\mathsf V^1_{U2})^2=\mathsf 1_{32}$, the sum in Eq. <a href="#eq:Pi" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Pi">[eq:Pi]</a> is a trace, $$\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\rm o,e}\big|{}_{U2}\langle\mathrm e|\mathsf V^1_{U2}|\mathrm o\rangle_{U2}\big|^2\\
&=\tfrac14\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\Big(\mathsf 1_{32}+\frac{\mathsf H_{U2}}{E^+_{U2}}\Big)\mathsf V^1_{U2}\Big(\mathsf 1_{32}-\frac{\mathsf H_{U2}}{E^+_{U2}}\Big)\mathsf V^1_{U2}\Big]\\
&=\frac{16(q^2+M^2_{U2})}{(E^+_{U2})^2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{49}$$ and every excitation energy is $2E^+_{U2}$. With the gap equation in the vacuum, $2/G^P_{U2}=16\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7/E^+_{U2}$, and with $4(E^+_{U2})^2-4(q^2+M^2_{U2})=4|\Delta_{U2}|^2$, the amplitude entry becomes $$\begin{aligned}
\big[\mathsf R_{U2}(w_{U2})\big]_{11}&=16\!\int\!\frac{d^7q}{(2\pi)^7}\,
\frac{4|\Delta_{U2}|^2-w^2_{U2}}{E^+_{U2}\,[4(E^+_{U2})^2-w^2_{U2}]}\\
&=c^\sigma_{U2}(w_{U2})\big(4|\Delta_{U2}|^2-w^2_{U2}\big),
\end{aligned}
\tag{50}$$ with the positive function $c^\sigma_{U2}(w_{U2})=16\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7\,(E^+_{U2})^{-1}[4(E^+_{U2})^2
-w^2_{U2}]^{-1}$ below the threshold $2E^+_{U2}|_{q=0}$. At $\mu_{U2}=0$ the amplitude does not mix with the phase (the mixing entry is proportional to $\mu_{U2}$), so the amplitude mode has the mass $$m_{\sigma U2}=2|\Delta_{U2}|,
\tag{51}$$ exactly, for every bare mass $m_{U2}$ and in every regularization that is used consistently in the gap equation and in $\Pi_{U2}$ (R3-M4, R3-M12). For $m_{U2}=0$ it sits at the pair-breaking threshold $2|\Delta_{U2}|$, as the scalar meson of Nambu and Jona-Lasinio sits at twice the fermion mass ([Y. Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961](#ref-NJL1961)) and the amplitude mode of a superconductor at twice the gap ([Littlewood and Varma 1982](#ref-LittlewoodVarma1982)). For $m_{U2}>0$ the threshold is $2(m^2_{U2}+|\Delta_{U2}|^2)^{1/2}>2|\Delta_{U2}|$: $\sigma_{U2}$ is then a *bound state below the continuum, a stable particle* (Table III, Fig. 8a). The decomposition $4|\Delta_{U2}|^2-w^2_{U2}=(|\Delta_{U2}|^2/s_{U2})(4s_{U2}-w^2_{U2})-(m^2_{U2}/s_{U2})w^2_{U2}$ shows why: $\Delta_1$ points partly along the radial direction of the plane of $(M_{U2},\Delta_{U2})$, whose mode sits at the threshold $2s^{1/2}_{U2}$, and partly along the angular direction, which costs no energy at zero frequency. At finite density, $\lambda^P_{U2}=0.5$ and $\mu_{U2}$ from 0.49 to 0.60, the computed mass is the threshold $2|\Delta_{U2}|$ to six digits (Table V).

The amplitude mode is the new scalar boson that the task asks for: (i) it is a real scalar field, the radial oscillation $|\Delta_{U2}|\to|\Delta_{U2}|_{\min}+\sigma_{U2}$ of the pair field, a Lorentz scalar because $\Phi^+_{U2}$ is one; (ii) it is neutral under every generator of $U(2)_{U2}$, because $|\Phi^+_{U2}|$ is gauge invariant, and therefore under any phase that acts on the doublet as a whole; if the doublet carries no electric charge, as assumed here (a dark sector), the pair, $B_{U2A}$ and $\sigma_{U2}$ are electrically neutral, and the condensate is a superfluid, not a superconductor of electric charge; (iii) it exists only after the breaking: at $\Delta_{U2}=0$ the small oscillations of $\Phi^+_{U2}$ form one complex field of charge 1, with two real components of equal mass, and only the minimum at $|\Delta_{U2}|\neq0$ separates a radial direction (mass $2|\Delta_{U2}|$) from the angular one (the Goldstone mode, absorbed by $B_{U2A}$); (iv) it carries the pairing interaction in the broken phase: the interaction of two fermions in the channel of $\Phi^+_{U2}$, summed over all chains of fermion bubbles, is $$\mathcal V^\sigma_{U2}(w_{U2})=-\big[\mathsf R_{U2}(w_{U2})^{-1}\big]_{11},
\tag{52}$$ the propagator of $\sigma_{U2}$, whose pole is $m_{\sigma U2}$. At zero frequency it is $-[\partial^2\Omega_{U2}/\partial\Delta_1^2]^{-1}$, negative (attractive) at every minimum tested, and $10.9$ times the bare contact interaction $-G^P_{U2}/2$ at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.05$ in the vacuum ($12.7$ with $m_{U2}=0.1$, $9.34$ at $\mu_{U2}=0.56$, $\lambda^P_{U2}=0.5$; R3-M11). In this precise sense the pairing in the superfluid is mediated by the scalar $\sigma_{U2}$. It is *not* the cause of the first pairing: before the breaking there is no $\sigma_{U2}$, and the attraction that forms the condensate is the contact term $G^P_{U2}$ (or, at a deeper level, the exchange of the $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons, Sec. VIII E).

### D. Stiffness, Meissner effect and the mass of $B_{U2A}$

*Spatial stiffness.* A condensate with a uniform phase gradient, $\Delta_{U2}\exp(i\vec Q_{U2}\cdot\vec x)$, pairs $\psi^1_{U2}$ at $\vec q+\vec Q_{U2}/2$ with $\psi^2_{U2}$ at $-\vec q+\vec Q_{U2}/2$. At $\mu_{U2}=0$ the eigenvalues of the corresponding Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix are, each eightfold (R3-C4), $$\pm\Big[q^2+\tfrac14Q^2_{U2}+s_{U2}\pm\big((\vec q\cdot\vec Q_{U2})^2+|\Delta_{U2}|^2Q^2_{U2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]^{1/2}.
\tag{53}$$ Expanding the sum of the two branches to second order in $Q_{U2}$ and averaging $(\vec q\cdot\vec Q_{U2})^2$ over the directions in seven dimensions, $q^2Q^2_{U2}/7$, gives the change of the fermion term, $-2Q^2_{U2}\int d^7q/(2\pi)^7(\tfrac67q^2+M^2_{U2})(E^+_{U2})^{-3}$. In the normal state ($\Delta_{U2}=0$) a phase gradient only shifts all momenta by $\vec Q_{U2}/2$, which cannot change the energy; with the cutoff on $q$ it does, by the surface term $-2Q^2_{U2}\,a^{(7)}_{U2}/[7(1+M^2_{U2})^{1/2}]$ (R3-M1: $\int_0^1q^6(\tfrac67q^2+M^2_{U2})\varepsilon^{-3}_{U2}dq
=[7(1+M^2_{U2})^{1/2}]^{-1}$), an artefact of the regularization, which we subtract. The physical cost, $\tfrac12(f^s_{U2})^2Q^2_{U2}$, defines the spatial stiffness; for $m_{U2}=0$ (R3-M5) $$(f^s_{U2})^2=4a^{(7)}_{U2}\int_0^1q^6\,\tfrac67q^2\Big[\frac1{q^3}-\frac1{(q^2+|\Delta_{U2}|^2)^{3/2}}\Big]dq>0.
\tag{54}$$

*Time-like stiffness.* A phase that rotates in time, $\partial_4\theta_{U2}\neq0$, is equivalent to a shift of the chemical potential by $-\partial_4\theta_{U2}/2$ (the pair has fermion number 2), so $\tfrac12(f^t_{U2})^2=-\tfrac18\partial^2\Omega_{U2}/\partial\mu^2_{U2}$ at fixed $\Delta_{U2}$, i.e. (R3-M6) $$(f^t_{U2})^2=2a^{(7)}_{U2}\int_0^1q^6|\Delta_{U2}|^2\big[(E^+_{U2})^{-3}+(E^-_{U2})^{-3}\big]dq.
\tag{55}$$ In (R7) the two differ: for small $|\Delta_{U2}|$ in the vacuum, Eqs. <a href="#eq:fs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fs">[eq:fs]</a> and <a href="#eq:ft" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:ft">[eq:ft]</a> give $(f^s_{U2})^2\to\tfrac97a^{(7)}_{U2}|\Delta_{U2}|^2$ and $(f^t_{U2})^2\to a^{(7)}_{U2}|\Delta_{U2}|^2$, so $f^s_{U2}/f^t_{U2}\to(9/7)^{1/2}$; the computed ratio grows from $1.136$ at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.005$ to $1.196$ at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.20$ (Table IV). A Lorentz-invariant vacuum must have $f^s_{U2}=f^t_{U2}$: the difference is the Lorentz violation of the cutoff on the seven-dimensional momentum. *In (R8)* the time-like stiffness is computed from the Euclidean form of the fermion term: $E^+_{U2}+E^-_{U2}$ is, up to a constant, the integral over the frequency $\kappa^{(4)}_{U2}$, divided by $2\pi$, of the logarithm of $[(\kappa^{(4)}_{U2})^2+(E^+_{U2})^2][(\kappa^{(4)}_{U2})^2+(E^-_{U2})^2]=(\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2}+\mu^2_{U2})^2
-4\varepsilon^2_{U2}\mu^2_{U2}$, with $\kappa^2_{U2}=q^2+(\kappa^{(4)}_{U2})^2$. The second derivative with respect to $\mu_{U2}$ at $\mu_{U2}=0$ gives $(f^t_{U2})^2=8\int d^8\kappa_{U2}/(2\pi)^8\,[\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2}-2\varepsilon^2_{U2}]/(\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2})^2$. Averaged over the directions of the 8-momentum ($q^2\to\tfrac78\kappa^2_{U2}$) the numerator is $2|\Delta_{U2}|^2-[\tfrac34\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2}]$; the bracket gives a part that survives at $\Delta_{U2}=0$, i.e. a stiffness of the normal state, which a regularization that respects the gauge symmetry (such as the continuation in the number of dimensions) sets to zero, exactly as the surface term above. What remains is the same for space-like and time-like gradients, by Lorentz invariance (R3-M7): $$\begin{aligned}
f^2_{U2}&=16|\Delta_{U2}|^2\!\int_{\kappa_{U2}<1}\!\frac{d^8\kappa_{U2}}{(2\pi)^8}\frac{1}{(\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2})^2}\\
&=16a^{(8)}_{U2}|\Delta_{U2}|^2\int_0^1\frac{\kappa^7_{U2}\,d\kappa_{U2}}{(\kappa^2_{U2}+s_{U2})^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{56}$$ Both stiffnesses are positive at every minimum: the condensate resists a twist of its phase.

*The mass of $B_{U2A}$.* By Eq. <a href="#eq:Dpair" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Dpair">[eq:Dpair]</a> the phase of the pair enters the effective Lagrangian only in the gauge-covariant combination $\partial_A\theta_{U2}+g'_{U2}B_{U2A}$ (the pair has the charge 1). In (R8), $$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L^{\rm eff}_{U2}={}&\tfrac12f^2_{U2}\,\eta^{AC}_{1671}\big(\partial_A\theta_{U2}+g'_{U2}B_{U2A}\big)\\
&\times\big(\partial_C\theta_{U2}+g'_{U2}B_{U2C}\big)+\dots
\end{aligned}
\tag{57}$$ In the unitary gauge, $\Theta^0_{U2}=-\theta_{U2}$, the phase disappears and leaves the mass term $\tfrac12g'^2_{U2}f^2_{U2}\,\eta^{AC}_{1671}B_{U2A}B_{U2C}$: $$m_{BU2}=g'_{U2}\,f_{U2},
\tag{58}$$ the Meissner effect of $U(1)_{U2}$; the Goldstone mode becomes the longitudinal state of the massive vector $B_{U2A}$. The three $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons stay massless: the condensate is an $SU(2)_{U2}$ singlet, $SU(2)_{U2}$ is unbroken, and its gauge invariance forbids a mass term. Unlike the electroweak case there is no mixing of $B_{U2A}$ with $\boldsymbol W_{U2A}$ (Sec. IV). In (R7) the temporal and spatial components of $B_{U2A}$ would get the different masses $g'_{U2}f^t_{U2}$ and $g'_{U2}f^s_{U2}$, again the Lorentz violation of that cutoff. Table IV and Fig. 8b give the numbers.

*The relativistic superfluid at finite density.* If $U(1)_{U2}$ were a global symmetry, the Goldstone mode would be a sound wave with the velocity $v^G_{U2}=f^s_{U2}/f^t_{U2}$, where now $(f^t_{U2})^2$ includes the change of $\Delta_{U2}$ with $\mu_{U2}$ (the full compressibility, which the random-phase approximation contains). At weak coupling (R3-M10, Table V, Fig. 8c) two classic results hold in seven space dimensions: the whole density is superfluid at zero temperature, $(f^s_{U2})^2=n_{U2}/(4\mu_{U2})$ (the relativistic form of Leggett’s theorem ([Leggett 1998](#ref-Leggett1998); [Son 2002](#ref-Son2002)); the factor $\tfrac14$ because the phase of the pair is twice the phase of a fermion), within 1 per cent for $\Delta_{U2}<0.02$; and $(v^G_{U2})^2=1/7$, the sound speed of a gas without a scale in seven space dimensions (pressure proportional to $\mu^8_{U2}$), within 1 per cent where $z_{U2}=\Delta_{U2}\,d\ln\Delta_{U2}/d\mu_{U2}<0.1$. Where the gap grows steeply with $\mu_{U2}$ the condensation energy adds to the compressibility and lowers the velocity, roughly by $z_{U2}^2$.

### E. Static potentials between two members of the doublet

Two heavy (static) members of the doublet exchange the gauge bosons. In seven space dimensions a massless vector boson gives two unit charges the potential $V^{\rm C}_{U2}(\varrho_{U2})=\Gamma_{\rm E}(5/2)/(4\pi^{7/2}\varrho^5_{U2})$ at the distance $\varrho_{U2}$ (the potential of a unit point charge in seven dimensions; $\Gamma_{\rm E}(5/2)=3\sqrt\pi/4$), and a vector boson of mass $m_{BU2}$ multiplies it by $\phi^{\rm Y}_{U2}(m_{BU2}\varrho_{U2})=\exp(-m_{BU2}\varrho_{U2})[(m_{BU2}\varrho_{U2})^2+3m_{BU2}\varrho_{U2}+3]/3$, which solves the radial equation of the massive field in seven dimensions and equals 1 at $\varrho_{U2}=0$ (R3-G7). Like charges repel. With the Pauli matrices $\boldsymbol\tau^{(1)}_{U2}$, $\boldsymbol\tau^{(2)}_{U2}$ acting on the two sources, $$\begin{aligned}
V^{\rm pair}_{U2}(\varrho_{U2})={}&\Big[g^2_{U2}\,\tfrac14\boldsymbol\tau^{(1)}_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau^{(2)}_{U2}
+(g'_{U2}y_{U2})^2\\
&\times\phi^{\rm Y}_{U2}(m_{BU2}\varrho_{U2})\Big]V^{\rm C}_{U2}(\varrho_{U2}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}$$ with $\tfrac14\boldsymbol\tau^{(1)}_{U2}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau^{(2)}_{U2}=-\tfrac34$ in the antisymmetric (singlet) state of the pair and $+\tfrac14$ in the three symmetric (triplet) states (R3-B1: the sum of the three products of Pauli matrices is twice the exchange of the two sources minus the unit). Before the breaking ($\phi^{\rm Y}_{U2}=1$) the singlet attracts if $(g'_{U2}y_{U2})^2<\tfrac34g^2_{U2}$, i.e. $(g'_{U2}/g_{U2})^2<3$; after the breaking the repulsion by $B_{U2A}$ is screened beyond the distance $1/m_{BU2}$, while the attraction by the massless $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons remains (Fig. 9). The exchange of $\boldsymbol W_{U2A}$ is thus an attraction in exactly the channel of the spin-zero pair; in this paper its effect on the gap is represented by the contact coupling $G^P_{U2}$.

## IX. What has been established, and what has not

### A. Established

Every statement in the following list is a theorem proved in the text or a result verified by the programs of Appendix C; the label of each machine check is given in parentheses, and Appendix B lists all of them with their results. All 55 checks of the program pass.

1.  On the $7{+}1$ spacetime a single Dirac field has no Lorentz-scalar pair, because both charge-conjugation matrices are symmetric; a doublet has exactly two, $\Phi^\pm_{U2}$, antisymmetric in the doublet index; $\Phi^+_{U2}$ is the pair of total spin zero and orbital angular momentum zero; the pairs join equal chiralities (R3-A1 to R3-A14).

2.  The unitary transformations of the doublet index that preserve $\Phi^+_{U2}$ form $SU(2)$; with the fermion number they form $U(2)_{U2}$. The covariant derivative <a href="#eq:D" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:D">[eq:D]</a> is covariant, its field strength transforms by conjugation, and the pair is an $SU(2)_{U2}$ singlet of $U(1)_{U2}$ charge 1 (R3-A9, R3-G1 to R3-G6). The Dirac doublet is free of gauge anomalies; a chiral doublet cannot carry the gauged $U(1)_{U2}$ (R3-B2).

3.  The mean-field Dirac equation has the explicit spinor solutions <a href="#eq:spinors" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:spinors">[eq:spinors]</a> with the quasi-particle energies <a href="#eq:Epm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Epm">[eq:Epm]</a>; the spin-zero pair opens the full gap at a Fermi surface, the pseudoscalar pair only $\Delta_{U2}q^F_{U2}/\mu_{U2}$ (R3-C1 to R3-C6, R3-U1).

4.  The effective potential <a href="#eq:Omega" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Omega">[eq:Omega]</a> has a Mexican-hat minimum, which breaks $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$, in the vacuum if and only if $\lambda^P_{U2}>\max(1,\lambda^S_{U2})$ (critical couplings $90\pi^4\Lambda^{-6}_{U2}$ and $288\pi^4\Lambda^{-6}_{U2}$ in the two regularizations), and at finite density for every attraction, with the weak-coupling law <a href="#eq:bcs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bcs">[eq:bcs]</a>; a density can drive a first-order transition from the massive symmetric vacuum to the superfluid (R3-P1 to R3-P12).

5.  After the breaking: a Goldstone mode, absorbed by $B_{U2A}$, which acquires the mass $g'_{U2}f_{U2}$; massless $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons; and the neutral scalar $\sigma_{U2}$ with $m_{\sigma U2}=2|\Delta_{U2}|$ exactly in the vacuum without scalar coupling, a bound state below the pair-breaking threshold when the fermions have a bare mass, whose propagator is the pairing interaction of the broken phase. At finite density the stiffness obeys Leggett’s theorem and the Goldstone velocity is $7^{-1/2}$ at weak coupling (R3-M1 to R3-M12).

6.  No symbol of this paper denotes two objects, here or in the rest of the project, and the documents are self-contained (R3-S1 to R3-S6 and the checks of self-containedness).

### B. Not established

1.  *Beyond mean field.* All results on the potential and the modes are of mean-field and random-phase order. With two flavours there is no small parameter that controls the corrections. The transition at the critical coupling should have the exponents of mean-field theory, because the order parameter lives in eight dimensions, above the upper critical dimension four of a Ginzburg–Landau theory; this has not been verified, and it would not make the mean-field values of $\Delta_{U2}$ exact.

2.  *Renormalizability.* The theory is not renormalizable: $G^S_{U2}$ and $G^P_{U2}$ have the dimension mass$^{-6}$, $g_{U2}$ and $g'_{U2}$ mass$^{-2}$, so every order of perturbation theory brings higher powers of the cutoff. The cutoff $\Lambda_{U2}$ is part of the model, which is an effective theory below it. The results that do not depend on the regularization are the existence of a critical coupling, the Mexican hat, the Cooper instability, the Goldstone mode, $m_{\sigma U2}=2|\Delta_{U2}|$, $m_{BU2}=g'_{U2}f_{U2}$, the massless $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons, Leggett’s theorem and $(v^G_{U2})^2=1/7$. The numbers in units of $\Lambda_{U2}$ depend on it: the critical couplings differ by the factor $3.2$ between (R7) and (R8), the vacuum gaps by the factor $1.44$ at $\lambda^P_{U2}=1.05$, and (R7) breaks Lorentz invariance ($f^s_{U2}\neq f^t_{U2}$). Whether an eight-dimensional theory of this kind can be made renormalizable is not addressed here.

3.  *The gauge fields in the gap equation.* The exchange of $\boldsymbol W_{U2A}$ (attractive in the singlet) and of $B_{U2A}$ (repulsive) is not included in the gap equation; the contact coupling $G^P_{U2}$ stands for the net attraction. The couplings $g_{U2}$ and $g'_{U2}$ enter only through the masses and the static potentials of Sec. VIII.

4.  *Finite temperature.* Only zero temperature is computed; the critical temperature is not.

5.  *The neutralizing background* at finite density is assumed, not derived.

6.  *Global anomalies* of a chiral doublet are not examined; they do not concern the Dirac doublet, which admits a gauge-invariant mass.

7.  *Physics in three space dimensions.* The model lives on the $7{+}1$ spacetime with momenta in all seven space dimensions; how it would appear in a world with three large space dimensions is not addressed, and gravity is not included.

### C. Where the result differs from the wording of the task

1.  *A “scalar gauge boson” does not exist.* A gauge boson is the quantum of a connection, a one-form; its quanta are vectors. The task asks for an electrically neutral scalar boson of the new group that appears only after the breaking and mediates the pairing. The model has two neutral bosons that exist as particles only after the breaking: the massive vector $B_{U2A}$ (a gauge boson, the analogue of the Z boson) and the scalar $\sigma_{U2}$ (not a gauge boson, the analogue of the Higgs boson). The scalar is the one that carries the pairing interaction, in the sense of Eq. <a href="#eq:Vsigma" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Vsigma">[eq:Vsigma]</a>.

2.  *“A new group”.* As an abstract group, $U(2)_{U2}$ is the group of the electroweak theory. What is new is its action on an internal index of 16-component fermions of the $7{+}1$ spacetime, the reason for it (Theorem 1), and the field that breaks it (a pair of fermions).

3.  *“Mediated”.* The scalar mediates the pairing in the broken phase; the attraction that forms the condensate in the first place is the contact coupling (or the exchange of the $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons).

4.  *Superfluid or superconductor.* The pair carries the gauged charge of $U(1)_{U2}$, so with respect to $U(1)_{U2}$ the condensate is a superconductor (it expels the field of $B_{U2A}$); with respect to electromagnetism, under which the doublet is assumed neutral, it is a superfluid.

## X. Conclusions

On the $7{+}1$ spacetime the symmetry of the charge-conjugation matrices forbids a spin-zero pair of a single Dirac field: the invariant form of the eight-dimensional spinor of Spin(7) is symmetric, and Fermi statistics then requires a second, flavour index in the antisymmetric combination. The group that preserves that combination is $SU(2)$; with the fermion number it is $U(2)_{U2}$, and gauging it with the covariant derivative <a href="#eq:D" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:D">[eq:D]</a>, modelled on the electroweak theory, gives a consistent, anomaly-free gauge theory of a Dirac doublet. With the quadratic and quartic self-interactions of Eq. <a href="#eq:L" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L">[eq:L]</a> the self-interacting Dirac equation <a href="#eq:EL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:EL">[eq:EL]</a> has, in mean field, the explicit spinor solutions <a href="#eq:spinors" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:spinors">[eq:spinors]</a>, and the effective potential <a href="#eq:Omega" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Omega">[eq:Omega]</a> of the superfluid has a Mexican-hat minimum that breaks $U(2)_{U2}\to SU(2)_{U2}$: in the vacuum when the pair coupling exceeds both the critical value and the scalar coupling, at finite density for any attraction, and, if the scalar channel wins in the vacuum, by a first-order transition driven by density. The breaking produces a massive neutral vector boson, $m_{BU2}=g'_{U2}f_{U2}$, leaves the $SU(2)_{U2}$ bosons massless, and creates a neutral scalar, $\sigma_{U2}$, of mass exactly $2|\Delta_{U2}|$ in the vacuum, bound below the continuum when the fermions have a mass; its propagator is the pairing interaction of the broken phase. The model is an effective theory below its cutoff; the results that do not depend on the regularization are listed in Sec. IX.

## Appendix C: Programs, data, and how to reproduce every number

### A. Programs

All computations are done by the Rust program `dark_u2` in the folder `code/dark-u2-rs` of `Revision_3`; the figures are drawn by a Python script, and the documents are assembled by Python scripts that insert every number from the data files, so that no computed number is typed by hand. The program has nine modules: `algebra` (the Dirac matrices, the pairs, the doublet, the anomaly coefficients; exact integer and Gaussian-integer arithmetic, ranks modulo the prime $2^{61}-1$), `gauge` (covariance of $D_{U2A}$ with random fields at a point, derivatives by fourth-order central differences; the static potentials), `bdg` (the Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix, its spectrum and spinors), `potential` (the effective potential, the gap and mass equations, the minima), `modes` (the random-phase approximation, the stiffness), `integrate` (adaptive Gauss–Legendre quadrature with 20 and 40 nodes, relative tolerance $10^{-15}$), `rsym` (real symmetric matrices and their cyclic Jacobi diagonalization), `checks` (the reports) and `main`. It uses the library crate `code/spacetimes-rs` of the repository for complex numbers, integer matrices and their rank, Gauss–Legendre nodes, Brent’s root finder, a parallel map and the writing of data files.

Numerical methods. Near a Fermi surface the integrals over $q$ use the variable $\mathrm{arsinh}(\xi^+_{U2}/|\Delta_{U2}|)$, in which the integrands are smooth for every gap however small; $E^-_{U2}$ is then computed as $|\Delta_{U2}|\cosh$ of that variable, without rounding error. The gap equation is solved by Brent’s method in $\ln|\Delta_{U2}|$ (gaps down to $10^{-300}$); the global minimum over $M_{U2}$ and $\Delta_{U2}$ is found on the profile $\min_{M_{U2}}\Omega_{U2}$ with the gap solved at each $M_{U2}$, by golden-section search and a final solution of the mass equation. The polarization uses Gauss–Legendre panels refined around the Fermi momentum, with $\vec q$ along the coordinate $x1$ (the integrands depend on the length of $\vec q$ only), and the eigenvectors of the $32\times32$ matrix at each node. The mass of $\sigma_{U2}$ is the zero of $\det\mathsf R_{U2}(w_{U2})$ below the threshold, or the threshold itself.

### B. Commands

From the folder `Revision_3` of the repository (a Rust toolchain for the edition 2021, Python 3 with NumPy and Matplotlib, and for the documents a TeX distribution with REVTeX 4.2, pandoc and XeLaTeX):

    cargo run --release --manifest-path code/dark-u2-rs/Cargo.toml -- all
    python3 code/scripts/make_figures.py
    bash code/scripts/check_all.sh

The first command writes every data file of the folder `data` (in a few seconds); the commands `algebra`, `gauge`, `bdg`, `potential` and `modes` in place of `all` run one part. The second draws the nine figures. The third runs both, then the checks of the symbols and of self-containedness, writes the fragments of the documents, and builds this paper and the provenance documents.
